Three British lives: Keynes, Bolton and Smith, 1883 to today

John Maynard Keynes, Anthony Bolton and Terry Smith on one line, against Britain's history and markets: 144 years and 11 market storms. Their lives never overlap: Keynes died in 1946, four years before Bolton was born.

Open the interactive UK timeline

Every storm since 1914, and what each of them did

The exchange shuts (31 Jul 1914 to 4 Jan 1915)

As war approaches, the London Stock Exchange closes on 31 July 1914 and stays shut for five months.

  • Keynes: Aged 31, a Cambridge economist. He writes an account of the City's crisis and joins the Treasury in January 1915.
  • Bolton: Not yet born.
  • Smith: Not yet born.

The post-war slump (1920 to 1921)

The post-war boom collapses. UK shares lose 9.3% in 1920 and unemployment soars.

  • Keynes: Speculating in currencies with borrowed money, he is wiped out by May 1920. By the end of 1922 he has cleared his debts.
  • Bolton: Not yet born.
  • Smith: Not yet born.

The Great Crash (1929 to 1931)

The Hatry fraud shakes London in September 1929, Wall Street crashes in October and the slump spreads. UK shares fall three years running; Britain leaves the gold standard in 1931.

  • Keynes: Commodity losses cut his own net worth to under £8,000. His King's fund falls 14.2% and 11.5% in the years to August 1930 and 1931, less than the market.
  • Bolton: Not yet born.
  • Smith: Not yet born.

The 1937-38 fall (1937 to 1938)

Recession, rearmament and the road to war. UK shares lose 15.7% in 1937 and 9.2% in 1938.

  • Keynes: His fortune falls from over £500,000 to under £200,000 and his King's fund loses 22.6% in the year to August 1938. He stays invested and explains why in his May 1938 memo.
  • Bolton: Not yet born.
  • Smith: Not yet born.

Britain alone (Sep 1939 to 1940)

War, Dunkirk and the Battle of Britain. UK shares lose 6.8% in 1940.

  • Keynes: Joins the Treasury as an adviser in August 1940. His King's fund loses 5.9% in the year to August 1940, while the index loses 18.8%.
  • Bolton: Not yet born.
  • Smith: Not yet born.

The 1973-74 crash (1973 to 1974)

An oil shock, a secondary banking crisis, the three-day week and soaring inflation. UK shares lose 28.7% in 1973 and 51.8% in 1974, then gain 152.1% in 1975.

  • Keynes: Died in 1946.
  • Bolton: In his early twenties at the merchant bank Keyser Ullman, learning the trade in the worst market since at least 1900.
  • Smith: Graduates from Cardiff with a First in 1974 and starts his career at Barclays.

Black Monday (19-20 Oct 1987)

Days after the Great Storm hits southern England, markets crash around the world. The FTSE 100 falls more than a fifth in two days, yet UK shares still end the year up 8.4%. Peak to bottom: about −36% (FTSE 100, to 9 Nov).

  • Keynes: Died in 1946.
  • Bolton: Eight years into running Special Situations.
  • Smith: Rated the top banking analyst in London.

The dot-com bust (Dec 1999 to Mar 2003)

The internet bubble bursts. The FTSE 100 halves from its peak at the end of 1999 to March 2003. Peak to bottom: about −53% (FTSE 100).

  • Keynes: Died in 1946.
  • Bolton: Had refused internet shares and lagged the market by almost 20% in the boom. The bust proves him right.
  • Smith: Floats Collins Stewart on the stock market in 2000, as chief executive.

The financial crisis (Sep 2007 to Mar 2009)

Northern Rock suffers the first run on a British bank in over a century. In 2008 Lehman Brothers fails and the government rescues the big banks. Peak to bottom: about −49% (FTSE 100, to 3 Mar 2009).

  • Keynes: Died in 1946.
  • Bolton: Hands over his fund at the end of 2007 and moves to mentoring.
  • Smith: Chief executive of Tullett Prebon, a broker between banks.

The COVID crash (Feb to Mar 2020)

Lockdown. UK shares fall 36% in 46 trading days and end the year down 9.8%. Peak to bottom: about −37% (FTSE 100, to 23 Mar).

  • Keynes: Died in 1946.
  • Bolton: Retired from fund management since 2014.
  • Smith: Fundsmith Equity gains 18.3% in the year, against 12.3% for world shares.

The gilt crisis (Sep to Oct 2022)

Inflation tops 10%, and September's mini-budget sends gilt yields soaring until the Bank of England steps in. UK shares end the year up 0.3%.

  • Keynes: Died in 1946.
  • Bolton: Retired.
  • Smith: Fundsmith Equity loses 13.8%, against a 7.8% fall for world shares.

Where their lives crossed

  • 2007: Bolton quotes Keynes. Stepping back from his fund, Bolton sets out his lessons in The Spectator under a headline invoking the wisdom of Keynes and Mark Twain.
  • 2010: Two launches. Bolton launches Fidelity China Special Situations in April; Smith launches Fundsmith Equity on 1 November.
  • 2014: Two exits. Bolton retires from running money in April; Smith leaves Tullett Prebon in September to focus on Fundsmith.

11 chapters

1883 to 1901: Victorian sunset

Britain runs the largest empire in history and London is the world's money market. Keynes is born in Cambridge in 1883. Gold is found in South Africa, the vote widens, and the reign of Queen Victoria ends in 1901.

  • Keynes: A Cambridge boy, then a scholar at Eton.
  • Bolton: Not yet born.
  • Smith: Not yet born.

1902 to 1913: The Edwardian years

Liberal governments bring old-age pensions and national insurance and curb the House of Lords. Keynes studies at King's, works at the India Office, and returns to Cambridge to teach economics.

  • Keynes: King's College, the India Office, then a Cambridge lecturer and editor.
  • Bolton: Not yet born.
  • Smith: Not yet born.

1914 to 1918: The Great War

War closes the London Stock Exchange for five months. Britain borrows heavily to fight. Keynes joins the Treasury and helps run the country's wartime finance.

  • Keynes: A senior Treasury official in his thirties.
  • Bolton: Not yet born.
  • Smith: Not yet born.

1919 to 1929: Peace, gold and speculation

Keynes quits the peace talks in protest, and his book about them makes him famous. He speculates, is wiped out, recovers, and starts running money for King's. Britain goes back to gold in 1925; the decade ends in crashes in London and New York.

  • Keynes: Bursar of King's, investor and famous critic of the peace.
  • Bolton: Not yet born.
  • Smith: Not yet born.

1930 to 1939: Slump and a new theory

The slump bites, Britain leaves the gold standard in 1931 and money becomes cheap. Keynes changes from forecasting the market to picking a few undervalued shares, writes The General Theory, survives a heart attack and a 1937-38 fall, and sets down his three principles.

  • Keynes: At the peak of his fame and his fortune, then ill.
  • Bolton: Not yet born.
  • Smith: Not yet born.

1940 to 1949: War and a new order

Britain stands alone in 1940, then wins the war with its allies. Keynes, now a peer, designs much of the post-war money system and dies in 1946. Labour builds the welfare state; the pound is devalued in 1949.

  • Keynes: Treasury adviser and negotiator; dies in April 1946.
  • Bolton: Not yet born.
  • Smith: Not yet born.

1950 to 1969: Never had it so good

Rationing ends, a new queen is crowned and shares boom in the 1950s. The pound is devalued again in 1967. Anthony Bolton is born in 1950 and Terry Smith in 1953.

  • Keynes: Died in 1946.
  • Bolton: A boy, then a pupil at Stowe and a Cambridge student.
  • Smith: A boy in East London.

1970 to 1979: The troubled seventies

Decimal money, Europe, oil shocks, strikes and inflation above 25%. UK shares lose half their value in 1974, then more than double in 1975. The decade ends with Margaret Thatcher and the end of exchange controls.

  • Keynes: Died in 1946.
  • Bolton: Learns the trade at Keyser Ullman and Schlesinger; joins Fidelity in December 1979.
  • Smith: Cardiff, then Barclays and an MBA.

1980 to 1999: Big Bang and the long boom

Privatisations, Big Bang in the City and a long bull market, interrupted by Black Monday in 1987 and the pound's exit from the ERM in 1992. The Bank of England becomes independent in 1997.

  • Keynes: Died in 1946.
  • Bolton: Builds Special Situations into the UK's best-known fund.
  • Smith: Top bank analyst, then sacked for Accounting for Growth; builds Collins Stewart.

2000 to 2009: Bust and crisis

The dot-com bubble bursts, then the first British bank run in over a century and the financial crisis. The FTSE 100 halves twice.

  • Keynes: Died in 1946.
  • Bolton: Proved right on technology; hands over his fund at the end of 2007.
  • Smith: Floats Collins Stewart and builds Tullett Prebon.

2010 to 2026: New funds, new shocks

Austerity, the Brexit vote, a pandemic, a gilt crisis and seven prime ministers in ten years. Smith founds Fundsmith; Bolton runs a China fund, then retires to compose. In January 2026 the FTSE 100 closes above 10,000 for the first time.

  • Keynes: Died in 1946.
  • Bolton: Runs a China fund until 2014, then composes music.
  • Smith: Runs Fundsmith: years well ahead of the market, then five behind.

The long run

  • 14.4% Keynes's King's fund a year compounded, 1922-46 (UK shares 9.0%)
  • 19.5% Bolton's Special Situations a year, 1979-2007 (UK market about 13.5%)
  • 13.8% Fundsmith Equity a year, Nov 2010-2025 (world shares 12.1%)
  • 8.7% UK shares a year with dividends, 1900-2025
