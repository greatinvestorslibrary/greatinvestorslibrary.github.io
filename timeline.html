Intersection of Lives: Buffett, Munger and Lynch, 1924 to today

Three investors side by side across a century of American history and markets: 103 years, 12 market storms and 16 moments their lives crossed.

Open the interactive timeline

Every storm since 1929, and what each of them did

The Great Crash (Sep 1929 to Jul 1932)

Wall Street crashes in October 1929 and keeps falling for almost three years. Banks fail and a quarter of workers lose their jobs. Peak to bottom: about −89% (Dow).

  • Munger: Aged 5 to 8 in Omaha.
  • Buffett: Born in August 1930, in the middle of it. In 1931 the bank where his father works fails.
  • Lynch: Not yet born.

The 1937 relapse (1937 to 1938)

Just as the economy recovers, a new recession hits. Stocks lose about 35% in 1937 alone.

  • Munger: A 13-year-old in Omaha.
  • Buffett: Six or seven, already selling things for a profit.
  • Lynch: Not yet born.

The Kennedy Slide (Dec 1961 to Jun 1962)

A sharp fall in the first half of 1962, with a one-day crash on May 28.

  • Munger: Starts his own fund this year and makes +30.1% while the Dow falls 7.6%.
  • Buffett: His partnership makes +13.9% against -7.6% for the Dow.
  • Lynch: 18, at Boston College.

The 1973-74 crash (Jan 1973 to Oct 1974)

Oil shock, inflation and Watergate. The 'Nifty Fifty' favourites collapse and the S&P 500 loses nearly half its value. Peak to bottom: about −48% (S&P 500).

  • Munger: His fund loses 31.9% and then 31.5%. He holds on, and in 1975 it rebounds 73.2%.
  • Buffett: Buys a stake in the Washington Post in 1973. Berkshire's shares fall 48.7% in 1974, yet he tells Forbes: "Now is the time to invest and get rich."
  • Lynch: Becomes Fidelity's director of research in 1974.

The Volcker squeeze (1980 to Aug 1982)

The Fed pushes interest rates to record highs to kill inflation. Two recessions follow, then a great bull market starts in August 1982.

  • Munger: Berkshire's vice chairman.
  • Buffett: Berkshire's shares rise 31.8% in 1981 while the S&P 500 falls 5%.
  • Lynch: Magellan opens to the public in 1981. Later he says: "Nobody called me to tell me about the recession in 1980 and 1981."

Black Monday (19 Oct 1987)

The Dow falls 22.6% in a single day, still its worst day ever. Yet the S&P 500 ends the year slightly up. Peak to bottom: about −23% (Dow, one day).

  • Munger: Berkshire's vice chairman. Berkshire ends 1987 up 4.6%.
  • Buffett: Had just invested $700 million in Salomon. Berkshire's shares drop from about $3,890 to $3,170 within days.
  • Lynch: On a golf trip in Ireland. Magellan loses about a third in two days; he cuts the trip short.

LTCM and Russia (Jul to Oct 1998)

Russia defaults and the giant hedge fund Long-Term Capital Management nearly collapses. The Fed organises a rescue by Wall Street banks. Peak to bottom: about −19% (S&P 500).

  • Munger: Berkshire's vice chairman.
  • Buffett: Offers, with AIG and Goldman Sachs, to buy LTCM. The offer is turned down.
  • Lynch: Retired from fund management since 1990.

The dot-com bust (Mar 2000 to Oct 2002)

The internet bubble bursts. The Nasdaq loses 78% and the S&P 500 about half. Peak to bottom: about −49% (S&P 500 (Nasdaq -78%)).

  • Munger: With Buffett, refuses to buy tech stocks he cannot value.
  • Buffett: Mocked in 1999 when Berkshire falls 19.9%; proved right in 2000 when it rises 26.6% as the market falls.
  • Lynch: Warns in a new edition of his book that very few dot-com investors "benefit from the full ride."

The financial crisis (Oct 2007 to Mar 2009)

Subprime loans go bad, Lehman Brothers fails and the financial system nearly freezes. Peak to bottom: about −57% (S&P 500).

  • Munger: Champions BYD; near the bottom, Daily Journal buys big-bank shares.
  • Buffett: Invests in Goldman Sachs and GE, writes "Buy American. I Am." and agrees to buy Burlington Northern.
  • Lynch: Long retired from fund management; in his 60s, at Fidelity.

The COVID crash (19 Feb to 23 Mar 2020)

A pandemic shuts the world. The fastest bear market ever, then a fast recovery. Peak to bottom: about −34% (S&P 500).

  • Munger: At 96, stays home from Berkshire's empty-arena meeting.
  • Buffett: Sells all of Berkshire's airline shares; later buys into five Japanese trading houses.
  • Lynch: In his 70s, at Fidelity.

The inflation bear (Jan to Oct 2022)

Inflation returns and the Fed raises rates faster than at any time since the 1980s. Peak to bottom: about −25% (S&P 500).

  • Munger: Steps down as Daily Journal chairman at 98.
  • Buffett: Buys Occidental, Chevron and Alleghany. Berkshire's shares rise 4.0% as the S&P 500 falls 18.1%.
  • Lynch: Still at Fidelity, in his late 70s.

The tariff shock (19 Feb to 8 Apr 2025)

Sweeping new tariffs send stocks down 10.5% in two days. Most are paused on April 9 and the market recovers to new records. Peak to bottom: about −19% (S&P 500).

  • Munger: Died in 2023.
  • Buffett: At his last meeting as CEO, says trade can be an act of war, then announces he will step down.
  • Lynch: Receives a lifetime achievement award at 81.

Where their lives crossed

  • 1930s: Same grocery, different years. As a teenager Munger worked at Buffett & Son, the Omaha grocery owned by Warren's grandfather. Warren worked there too, later. They did not meet.
  • 1957: A doctor's hunch. Dr. Edwin Davis invests $100,000 with Buffett because he reminds him of Charlie Munger. The Davises are determined to introduce them.
  • 1959: First meeting. Back in Omaha after his father's death, Munger meets Buffett at a dinner arranged by the Davis family. Buffett knows at once he has found someone to learn from.
  • c. 1960: Law as a hobby. Buffett tells Munger that law is fine as a hobby, but he could do better. Two years later Munger starts his own fund.
  • 1966: Partners in a store. With Sandy Gottesman they buy the Baltimore department store Hochschild, Kohn. It is a mistake, and an early lesson for both.
  • 1972: See's Candies. Munger persuades Buffett to pay up for a great business. See's becomes the model for Berkshire's later buys.
  • 1978: Vice chairman. Munger formally joins Berkshire as vice chairman: the start of 45 years side by side.
  • 1984: Superinvestors. Buffett's famous Columbia talk uses Munger's partnership record as proof that Graham's way works.
  • 1989: The phone call. Buffett phones Lynch at home to ask if he may quote One Up on Wall Street in his annual letter. Lynch's daughter takes the call.
  • 1994: The foreword. Lynch writes the foreword to The Warren Buffett Way.
  • 2008: BYD. Munger champions the Chinese carmaker BYD, and Berkshire buys about 10% of it.
  • 2015: Two letters. For Berkshire's 50th anniversary each writes his own letter to shareholders, side by side.
  • 2021: The greatest. Lynch calls Buffett the greatest investor of all time.
  • 2021: The slip. Munger lets slip at the annual meeting that Greg Abel will keep the culture. Buffett confirms Abel as his successor.
  • 2023: Goodbye, Charlie. Munger dies at 99. Buffett says Berkshire could not have been built to what it is without him.
  • 2026: Two jerseys. Berkshire retires a '60' jersey for Buffett and a '45' jersey for Munger at the first meeting run by Greg Abel.

A century in 11 chapters

1924 to 1929: The Roaring Twenties

Radio, cars and easy credit. Ordinary Americans discover the stock market, the first modern mutual fund opens in Boston, and Charlie Munger is born in Omaha. It ends in the crash of 1929.

  • Munger: A small boy in Omaha.
  • Buffett: Not yet born.
  • Lynch: Not yet born.

1930 to 1941: Depression childhoods

Banks fail, a quarter of workers lose their jobs and stocks lose most of their value. Washington answers with the New Deal and the SEC. Warren Buffett is born ten months after the crash. The decade ends with a world at war.

  • Munger: An Omaha schoolboy, then a maths student in Michigan.
  • Buffett: An Omaha boy already selling things for profit.
  • Lynch: Not yet born.

1942 to 1949: War and first trades

America fights and wins a world war, then builds the post-war order. Buffett buys his first stock at 11, Munger forecasts weather for the Army and talks his way into Harvard Law, and Peter Lynch is born near Boston.

  • Munger: Army weatherman, Harvard Law, then a young Los Angeles lawyer.
  • Buffett: A teenage businessman in Washington, then Wharton.
  • Lynch: A baby, then a small boy in Newton, Massachusetts.

1950 to 1959: Students of Graham

A booming, confident America; the Dow finally regains its 1929 peak in 1954. Buffett learns from Benjamin Graham, works for him, then starts his own partnership. Lynch loses his father and starts caddying. In 1959 Buffett and Munger meet.

  • Munger: A Los Angeles lawyer through a hard decade at home.
  • Buffett: Columbia, Omaha broker, Graham-Newman, then his own partnership.
  • Lynch: A boy caddying at a Boston-area golf club.

1960 to 1969: The go-go years

Kennedy, Vietnam, civil rights and the Moon. Wall Street falls for fast-trading 'go-go' funds. Buffett buys a struggling textile mill called Berkshire Hathaway; Munger starts his own firm and fund; Lynch goes from college to Fidelity.

  • Munger: Founds his law firm and runs his own investment partnership.
  • Buffett: Runs his partnership; takes control of Berkshire in 1965; closes the partnership in 1969.
  • Lynch: Boston College, a Fidelity internship, Wharton and the Army.

1970 to 1979: The lost decade

Watergate, oil shocks and inflation. Stocks go nowhere for a decade and crash in 1973-74. Buffett buys See's Candies, the Washington Post and GEICO at bargain prices; Munger's fund is battered, then recovers; Lynch takes over a tiny fund called Magellan.

  • Munger: Rides out a brutal 1973-74, winds up his fund and joins Berkshire as vice chairman in 1978.
  • Buffett: Runs Berkshire and buys great businesses cheaply.
  • Lynch: Fidelity analyst, research director, then Magellan's manager from 1977.

1980 to 1989: The great bull begins

Paul Volcker breaks inflation with sky-high rates, and a great bull market begins in 1982. Magellan becomes the most famous fund in America. Berkshire buys Nebraska Furniture Mart and Coca-Cola. Black Monday tests everyone in 1987.

  • Munger: Berkshire's vice chairman; also runs Wesco from 1984.
  • Buffett: Turns Berkshire into a conglomerate of great businesses.
  • Lynch: Builds Magellan from about $100 million to about $10 billion.

1990 to 1999: The bubble decade

The Cold War ends, the web arrives and tech stocks soar. Lynch quits at the top at 46. Buffett rescues Salomon and buys GEICO and General Re. Munger gives his most famous talks. By 1999 critics call Buffett out of touch.

  • Munger: Gives his landmark talks on worldly wisdom and human misjudgment.
  • Buffett: Builds Berkshire's insurance empire and refuses to chase tech.
  • Lynch: Retires from Magellan, writes two more books and turns to giving.

2000 to 2009: Bust, war and crisis

The dot-com bubble bursts, terrorists strike on 9/11, and in 2008 the financial system nearly collapses. Buffett warns about derivatives, pledges his fortune to charity and invests boldly in the panic, with Munger's China idea in the mix.

  • Munger: Vice chairman and sage; his sayings become a best-selling book.
  • Buffett: Warns early, then invests heavily in the 2008 panic.
  • Lynch: Vice chairman at Fidelity and a full-time philanthropist.

2010 to 2019: The long recovery

A slow recovery, then a long bull market led by technology. Buffett launches the Giving Pledge, receives the Medal of Freedom, buys Apple and wins his famous bet against hedge funds. Berkshire starts preparing for life after its founders.

  • Munger: In his 80s and 90s, still at Buffett's side and still giving big gifts.
  • Buffett: Buys huge businesses and Apple; names vice chairmen.
  • Lynch: Gives away money through the Lynch Foundation; loses his wife in 2015.

2020 to 2026: Pandemic, AI and farewells

A pandemic crash, artificial intelligence, war and new tariffs. Munger dies in 2023, 34 days short of 100. Buffett hands the CEO job to Greg Abel and then the chair to his son Howard. Lynch, in his 80s, is still at Fidelity.

  • Munger: Works into his late 90s; dies in November 2023.
  • Buffett: Steps back: CEO until 2025, chairman until September 2026.
  • Lynch: In his 80s, still meeting Fidelity's young analysts.
