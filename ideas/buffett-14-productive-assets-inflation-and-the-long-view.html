Warren Buffett: Productive assets, inflation and the long view

Wealth compounds in things that produce: businesses, farms, property. Claims on money and objects that produce nothing do not compound.

  • Three kinds of asset. Claims on currency, such as deposits and bonds, look safe and lose purchasing power quietly. Assets that produce nothing depend on a later buyer paying more. Productive assets keep yielding whatever happens to the currency. He chooses the third by a wide margin. (2011)
  • The gold cube. All the world's gold, about 170,000 tonnes, would make a cube 68 feet a side. At that year's price it equalled all the farmland in America plus sixteen Exxon Mobils, with about $1 trillion left over. A century on, the farms and companies will have produced enormous output. The cube will be the same cube. (2011)
  • The $114.75 example. His first share purchase, in 1942, cost $114.75. Put into a no-fee index fund with dividends reinvested, that sum would have reached $606,811 by early 2019. In gold it would have been about $4,200. (2018)
  • What holds up in inflation. The ideal asset can raise its prices with the currency and needs little fresh capital to do so. Farms, property and businesses such as See's pass both tests. (2011)
  • Cash: fortress, not home. He holds very large amounts of Treasury bills so that Berkshire can never be forced. In his last letter he also wrote that he will never prefer cash-equivalents to ownership of good businesses. Both statements hold together. (2003 · 2024)
  • The tailwind. Productivity gains have raised living standards for two centuries, and he expects that to continue. He says much of Berkshire's record came from this, as distinct from his own skill. (2015 · 2018 · 2020)
  • Japan. Stakes in five trading houses, bought from 2019 and largely funded by borrowing in yen, so the currency risk is neutral. Simple businesses, low prices, sensible managements. The position was disclosed on his ninetieth birthday, a late lesson in looking abroad. (2020 to 2024)
“You can fondle the cube, but it will not respond.”Warren Buffett, 2011 letter
“The babies being born in America today are the luckiest crop in history.”Warren Buffett, 2015 letter
“Never bet against America.”Warren Buffett, 2020 letter

Ask yourself

  • He writes about America. The portable idea is to own productive assets in an economy with rule of law and rising productivity. How does my own allocation look against that test?
  • What in my balance sheet depends on someone later paying a higher price, with nothing produced in between?
