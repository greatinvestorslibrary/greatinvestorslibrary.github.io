Warren Buffett: A share is a slice of a business

You are buying a claim on what a company will earn. The price on the screen is an offer from a stranger, and it carries no information about the business.

  • Pick businesses, not tickers. He buys on a view of how the company will do over many years. He holds no view on where the share price or the market goes next, and does not act as if he had one. (2021 · 2022)
  • The farm and the shop. In 1986 he bought a Nebraska farm, in 1993 a retail property in New York, knowing little about farming or real estate. He asked only two things: what will this asset earn over the years, and is the price sensible against that? Neither came with a daily quote, and he never missed having one. (2013)
  • Watch the field. If you can enjoy a weekend without checking the price of something you own, you can do the same on a weekday. Results on the ground decide the outcome; the price catches up later. (2013)
  • Count the earnings you never see. Your real income from a holding includes your share of the profit the company keeps. Accounts show only the dividend. At a business that reinvests well, the retained part is the more valuable half. (1999 · 2019 · 2020)
  • Popularity now, weight later. Over months, price measures how liked a share is. Over years, it measures what the business is worth. This is Graham's idea, and he repeats it thirty years on because nothing has replaced it. (2022 · 2023)
  • Two doors into the same room. Buying a whole company and buying 2% of a listed one are the same act of judgement. The listed route has two advantages: shares are sometimes offered at foolish prices, and a mistake can be sold in a day. (1995 · 2022)
  • Part of a gem beats all of a pebble. A small piece of an outstanding business is worth more than full control of an average one, and it is far less work. (2013 · 2014 · 2020)
  • Keep an honest yardstick. A rising market flatters everyone. The test is whether you beat a cheap index after costs and tax over several years, using a benchmark chosen in advance and never swapped after a bad stretch. (1997 · 2004 · 2009)
“Charlie and I are not stock-pickers; we are business-pickers.”Warren Buffett, 2021 letter
“Games are won by players who focus on the playing field”Warren Buffett, 2013 letter

Ask yourself

  • For each holding: could I describe what this company will probably earn five years from now, and why, without mentioning its share price?
  • If the exchange shut for three years tomorrow, which holdings would I be content to keep?
