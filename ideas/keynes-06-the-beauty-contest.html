John Maynard Keynes: The beauty contest

He compared the stock market to a newspaper contest where you win by guessing what others will pick, not what you think is best.

  • The contest. Readers had to pick the six prettiest faces from a hundred photographs. The prize went to whoever picked closest to the average choice. (General Theory, 1936)
  • Second and third guesses. So each player picks not the face he finds prettiest, but the one he thinks others will pick, and some go further still. (General Theory, 1936)
  • Professionals do the same. Many professional investors focus on guessing how market opinion will shift in the coming months, not on a business's long-run worth. (General Theory, 1936)
  • Your edge. Prices can drift far from value for a long time. Thinking about the business, not the crowd, is what lets you profit when they meet again. (CFA Institute, 2014)

Ask yourself

  • When did you last buy something because you thought others would want it?
  • How could the beauty contest create bargains for a patient investor?
