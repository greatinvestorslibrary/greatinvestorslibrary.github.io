John Templeton: The 1939 basket

As war broke out in Europe, he borrowed $10,000 and bought 100 shares in each of 104 companies trading at $1 or less. Nearly all of them made money.

  • The order. In 1939 he bought 100 shares in each of 104 companies selling at $1 or less, including 34 in bankruptcy. (Morningstar · Irish Times, 2008)
  • Only four failed. Just four of the 104 let him down; after repaying the loan he had a handsome profit. (Morningstar · Irish Times, 2008)
  • Why war helped. He reasoned that in wartime, things that were in surplus and unprofitable become scarce and profitable. (Morningstar)
  • A borrowed stake. He borrowed the money, a big risk for a young man; he spread it across 104 companies so no single failure could sink him. (Morningstar)

Ask yourself

  • Why did buying all 104 make more sense than picking five?
  • Would you borrow to invest? What could go wrong?
