Benjamin Graham: Mr Market

Picture a partner who offers every day to buy your share or sell you his, at a price that depends on his mood. You may take his offer or ignore it.

  • The parable. Mr Market turns up daily with a price. Some days he is euphoric and asks too much; some days gloomy and sells too cheap. (Book, 1973 · Buffett, 1987)
  • Ignore him freely. You are never obliged to trade. His offers are there to use when they suit you. (Buffett, 1987 · Book, 1973)
  • One meaning only. For a true investor, price swings mean one thing: a chance to buy wisely after sharp falls and sell wisely after big rises. Otherwise, forget the market. (Book, 1973)
  • Buffett's debt. Buffett wrote that this picture of Mr Market describes the attitude to price swings he finds most useful for investing. (Buffett, 1987)

Ask yourself

  • When did Mr Market last offer you a silly price, high or low?
  • Would you check a share's price every day if you owned the whole business?
