Charlie Munger: The inner life: envy, resentment, self-pity

Much of his life advice is about thoughts to refuse. A few emotions do more damage than bad luck, and they are under your control.

  • Three from Carson. He began with Johnny Carson's prescriptions for misery: mood-altering chemicals, envy and resentment. (HS 1986)
  • Resentment costs the carrier. He cites Disraeli, who learned to give up revenge as a motive, though he kept an outlet for resentment. A grudge mostly harms the person who carries it. (HS 1986)
  • Four to refuse. Envy, resentment, revenge and self-pity: he named them together as disastrous ways of thinking. (USC Law 2007)
  • Envy is everywhere. He said envy and jealousy are extreme in modern life. Expect them in others, and watch for them in yourself. (PHM 2005)
  • Defer the reaction. The standard cure for overactive hostility is to train yourself to delay your response. (PHM 2005)
  • Denial. The mind bends facts that are too painful to accept. Notice when you are doing it. (PHM 2005)
  • Light stress, heavy stress. Light stress can slightly improve performance; heavy stress causes dysfunction. Make big decisions when calm. (PHM 2005)
  • Being right can cost you. You can cause great offence by being right in a way that makes someone lose face in front of others. (USC Law 2007)
  • Avoid intense ideology. He warned that extremely intense ideology clouds the mind. (USC Law 2007)
  • How to bear hard times. Have low expectations, keep a sense of humour, and surround yourself with the love of friends and family. (Stanford 1996)
“Generally speaking, envy, resentment, revenge, and self-pity are disastrous modes of thought.”Charlie Munger, USC Law 2007

Ask yourself

  • Whose success do you watch most closely? What does watching it do to your decisions?
  • Is there a grudge or a regret you replay? What would change if you put it down this week?
