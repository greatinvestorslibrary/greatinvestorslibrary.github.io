Atsuto Sawakami: Think in decades

Look at least ten years ahead, he says, and ask how a company helps people live now and will help them in future.

  • Ten years, at least. Investors should look at least ten years ahead, and hold for about that long. (Toyo Keizai, 2014)
  • People's lives. Ask how a company supports people's lives today and how it will tomorrow; avoid businesses built on chasing money. (Toyo Keizai, 2014)
  • 185,000 a day. With the world's population growing by about 185,000 people a day, demand for energy, food, materials and water must rise. (Toyo Keizai, 2014)
  • Don't fear pessimism. He argued that 'Japan pessimism' was nothing to fear, and that it was the time for long-term investing. (Toyo Keizai, 2014)

Ask yourself

  • Which companies you know will matter more in ten years?
  • What would you own if you could not sell for a decade?
