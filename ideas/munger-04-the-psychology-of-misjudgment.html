Charlie Munger: The psychology of misjudgment

He built a list of 25 mental tendencies that lead sensible people into error, and used it as a checklist. The danger peaks when several act at once.

  • Three talks became one. Talks given in 1992, 1994 and 1995 became one. He rewrote it for the book in 2005, at 81, from memory and without fresh research. (PHM 2005)
  • Use it as a checklist. He ran through his own list, tendency by tendency, to explain events that puzzled him. Do the same before an important decision. (PHM 2005)
  • Lollapalooza. Extreme outcomes come from several tendencies pushing the same way. One of his examples is an airliner evacuation test, run in a darkened hangar with older volunteers, that ended in many injuries. (PHM 2005)
  • Authority can be fatal. People defer to whoever is in charge, even when that person is plainly wrong. In one of his examples, that deference ended in a plane crash. (PHM 2005)
  • Know it; don't abuse it. Not everything you know about psychology should be used to manipulate people. Anyone who sees through it will hate you for it. (Stanford 1996)
  • Crowd folly. Under some conditions people behave like lemmings. He said this explains much foolish thinking, in markets as elsewhere. (Roundtable 2000)
  • Priced like paintings. He described shares being valued partly like Rembrandt paintings: bought mostly because their prices had already risen so far. (Roundtable 2000)

Ask yourself

  • Take one decision you made in the last year. Which of the 25 tendencies were acting on you?
  • Where have three or more tendencies been pushing you the same way at once?
