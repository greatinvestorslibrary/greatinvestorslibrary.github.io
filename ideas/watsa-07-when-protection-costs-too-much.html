Prem Watsa: When protection costs too much

From 2010 he hedged all of Fairfax's shares against a deflationary slump that never came. The hedges cost billions before he removed them in 2016.

  • Fully hedged. From 2010 Fairfax hedged about 100% of its shares; by September 2016 the hedges were 112.7% of its holdings. (Press, 2016 · Nasdaq, 2017)
  • The cost. When removed in late 2016, the index shorts had cost about $1.2 billion that year alone. (Nasdaq, 2017)
  • A change of view. After the 2016 US election he judged the hedges no longer needed and took them off. (Press, 2016)
  • The lesson. Protection that works once can be too expensive the next time; the cost must be weighed. (Nasdaq, 2017)

Ask yourself

  • What protection are you paying for today?
  • How would you know if it costs more than it saves?
