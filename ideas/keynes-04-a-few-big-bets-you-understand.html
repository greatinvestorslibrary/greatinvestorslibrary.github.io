John Maynard Keynes: A few big bets you understand

He thought spreading money across many things you know little about was a mistake. Better a few large holdings you understand.

  • Know the business. In 1934 he told a colleague it is better to invest decent amounts in a few businesses you understand and whose managers you believe in. (Letter, 1934)
  • Two or three at a time. He reckoned that at any one moment only two or three companies earned his complete confidence. (Letter, 1934)
  • Concentrated in practice. His holdings grew from about 20 to over 60, yet at times more than half the fund sat in five shares. (CFA Institute, 2014)
  • A bold position. In 1936, 66% of the fund was in mining shares. (CFA Institute, 2014)
“put fairly large sums into enterprises which one thinks one knows something about”John Maynard Keynes, Letter to F. C. Scott, 1934

Ask yourself

  • How many companies do you understand well enough to bet big on?
  • What is the risk of concentration, and how did his third principle answer it?
