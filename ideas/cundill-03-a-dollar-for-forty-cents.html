Peter Cundill: A dollar for forty cents

He bought shares priced below the company's liquidation value: what it would be worth if it closed and sold everything.

  • Liquidation value. A share is cheap when its price is below what the business would fetch if wound up. (Morningstar · Book, 2011)
  • Margin of safety. That gap was the margin of safety he swore by. (Morningstar)
  • A dollar for forty cents. He popularised the idea of buying a dollar of value for 40 cents. (Morningstar)
  • Net-nets. His favourite was a share below working capital minus all debts, Graham's net-net. (Morningstar)
“buying a dollar for 40 cents”Peter Cundill, The idea he popularised, as reported by Morningstar Canada

Ask yourself

  • What would your largest holding fetch if it closed tomorrow?
  • How big a gap would you want?
