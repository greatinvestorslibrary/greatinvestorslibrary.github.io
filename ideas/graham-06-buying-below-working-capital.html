Benjamin Graham: Buying below working capital

His favourite bargain: shares selling for less than the company's current assets minus all its debts, giving nothing at all for factories or brands.

  • The method. Buy shares priced below net current asset value: current assets minus every liability, with fixed assets counted as zero. (Interview, 1976)
  • His own result. He said this method had earned about 20% a year over more than 30 years. (Interview, 1976)
  • They come and go. Such bargains grew scarce in the boom years, then returned after the 1973 to 1974 crash: in January 1976 he counted over 300, about a tenth of listed shares. (Interview, 1976)
  • Buy a group. He applied it to a spread of such shares, not one or two, so that the winners outweighed the disappointments. (Book, 1973)

Ask yourself

  • Why might a company sell for less than its cash and stock in hand?
  • What could make such a bargain a trap?
