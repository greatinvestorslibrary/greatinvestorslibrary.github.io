Charlie Munger: The Berkshire system, in his words

In his 2014 essay, written directly to Berkshire shareholders for the first time, he explained why the company had worked.

  • Four causes. He credited the constructive peculiarities of Buffett, the constructive peculiarities of the Berkshire system, good luck, and the intense devotion of some shareholders and admirers. (BRK 2014)
  • Focus, kept for 50 years. Buffett's decision to limit himself to a few kinds of activity, and keep at them for 50 years, worked like a lollapalooza. Munger compared it to Roger Federer's tennis. (BRK 2014)
  • Each company keeps its own systems. There is no group-wide personnel, stock option, incentive or retirement system. Subsidiaries run their own. (BRK 2014)
  • Keep head office small. Buffett set out to minimise the bad effects that almost always come from a large bureaucracy at headquarters. (BRK 2014)
  • Win/win. He wanted win/win results everywhere, for instance gaining loyalty by giving it. (BRK 2014)
  • A narrow job at the top. The chairman keeps few tasks: managing nearly all the security investments, writing a long, useful letter as he would want it as a passive shareholder, answering questions for hours, and admiring what others achieve. (BRK 2014)
  • Rare elsewhere. No other large company he knew had half these elements. Most have heavy bureaucracy and chief executives who arrive at about 59 and are soon retired by an age limit. (BRK 2014)
  • Successors. He named Ajit Jain and Greg Abel as proven performers. (BRK 2014)
“the weirdly intense, contagious devotion of some shareholders”Charlie Munger, BRK 2014

Ask yourself

  • Which of the companies you own gives managers real autonomy, and how do you know it is deserved?
  • How much unhurried time for reading and thinking do you protect each week?
