Benjamin Graham: Margin of safety

Pay well below what something is worth. The gap protects you from your own mistakes, bad luck and the unknown.

  • The central idea. He called the margin of safety the central concept of investment, the closing chapter of his book. (Book, 1973)
  • Every purchase justified. In his last interview: every purchase, and the price paid, should be justified by a margin of safety in value terms. (Interview, 1976)
  • Dollars for 40 cents. Buffett summed up Graham's school as buying a dollar of value for 40 cents, then waiting. (Buffett, 1984)
  • Safety in numbers. A margin of safety works on average, not every time, which is why he spread his money across many holdings. (Book, 1973)
“a margin of safety, in value terms, to protect his commitment”Benjamin Graham, Financial Analysts Journal interview, 1976

Ask yourself

  • What is your cautious estimate of one company's value? How far below it is the price?
  • What could go wrong that your margin would have to absorb?
