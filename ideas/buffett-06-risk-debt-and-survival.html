Warren Buffett: Risk, debt and survival

Risk is the chance of losing capital for good. Price swings are something else. Stay in a position where no single event can end the game.

  • The one rule. Among everything that changed at Berkshire over six decades, he names one investment rule that has not and will not: do not put capital at risk of permanent loss. (2023)
  • Volatility is not risk. Risk is the probability of ending your holding period with less purchasing power. Over a day or a year, shares are riskier than cash. Over decades, cash and bonds are the riskier choice. The horizon decides which is safe. (2011 · 2014)
  • Investors make shares risky themselves. By trading actively, trying to time moves, diversifying too little, paying high fees and borrowing. Each can turn a good long-run asset into a poor result. (2014)
  • One zero is enough. A long run of fine numbers multiplied by a single zero is zero. Leverage wins most of the time, which is why it is addictive, and the rare loss is total. (2010)
  • Never rely on strangers. Keep so much liquidity that any conceivable cash need is small beside it. This costs return in ordinary years. It buys sleep, survival and the means to purchase when others must sell. (2009 · 2010 · 2021)
  • The tide. Only when conditions turn do you learn who was running without protection. Calm years hide weak practice. (2001)
  • The Noah rule. He had foreseen the risk of a mega-catastrophe and done too little about it. Forecasting the flood earns nothing. The ark does. (2001)
  • Exposure, not experience. Judge a risk by what could happen. A quiet record says little about a danger that has not struck yet. (2001)
  • Look for hidden links. Many small, unrelated risks are safe to carry. One concentrated exposure, or several that turn out to move together, is not. (2001 · 2005)
  • History-fed formulas. Models built on past data give precise answers and false comfort when the conditions that produced the data have changed. He warns in particular against handing risk decisions to people with formulas. (2008)
  • Derivatives. Hidden leverage, chains of counterparties, and profits booked on models years before any cash arrives. He wound down General Re's book and admitted he took too long. (2002 · 2003)
  • Small odds, long time. A one-in-thirty chance each year becomes about 97% over a century. A risk you would dismiss in any single year is close to certain across an investing life. (2015)
  • Low rates are no excuse. When safe yields are poor, the temptation is to accept weaker credit or longer maturities. He holds Treasury bills and waits. (2003 · 2019 · 2020)
“Never risk permanent loss of capital.”Warren Buffett, 2023 letter
“Volatility is far from synonymous with risk.”Warren Buffett, 2014 letter
“Predicting rain doesn't count; building arks does.”Warren Buffett, 2001 letter
“derivatives are financial weapons of mass destruction”Warren Buffett, 2002 letter
“credit is like oxygen”Warren Buffett, 2010 letter

Ask yourself

  • What single event would do permanent damage to my finances? What have I built against it, as opposed to merely predicted?
  • Which of my positions would hurt together in the same bad year?
