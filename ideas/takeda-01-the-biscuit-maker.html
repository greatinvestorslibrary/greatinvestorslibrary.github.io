Wahei Takeda: The biscuit maker

He was a confectioner first. He turned a sweet sold loose by weight into a bagged, machine-made product, and built Japan's biggest tamago boro maker.

  • A confectioner's son. Born in Aichi in 1933 to a family of sweet makers, he was his father's right hand while still a teenager after the war. (MUFG, 2024)
  • A simple idea. Tamago boro was sold loose by weight in sweet shops. He saw it could be sold in bags, and automated production before rivals did. (MUFG, 2024)
  • Takeda Seika. He founded the company in 1952; it became Japan's largest tamago boro maker, also known for the Mugi Fah wafer. (Obituary, 2016 · MUFG, 2024)
  • A poor craftsman. His interviewer described him as a once-poor confectioner who became Japan's biggest individual investor. (Book, 2008)
