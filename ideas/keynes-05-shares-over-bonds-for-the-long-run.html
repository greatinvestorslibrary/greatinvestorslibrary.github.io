John Maynard Keynes: Shares over bonds, for the long run

He moved a centuries-old college endowment from land and bonds into shares, decades before other institutions.

  • Ahead of his time. He moved King's into shares while most institutions still relied on bonds and property. (Chambers & Dimson)
  • Shares for the long run. For investors with very long horizons, he favoured shares over bonds. (CFA Institute, 2014)
  • Look abroad. By the late 1930s his fund held less in Britain than in the US or Africa. (CFA Institute, 2014)
  • Value and dividends. He leaned towards cheap shares with high dividend yields. One famous buy, the mining group Union Corporation, cost 30% less than its estimated break-up value. (CFA Institute, 2014)

Ask yourself

  • Why might a fund that must last for centuries prefer shares to bonds?
  • What does buying below break-up value protect you from?
