Benjamin Graham: Seven tests for the defensive investor

For investors who want safety, he set seven plain tests: size, strong finances, steady earnings, dividends, growth, and moderate prices against earnings and assets.

  • Size and strength. A large company, with current assets at least twice current liabilities and long-term debt no larger than working capital. (Book, 1973)
  • Record. Earnings in each of the past ten years, dividends paid without a break for twenty, and earnings up at least a third over ten years. (Book, 1973)
  • Price. No more than 15 times average earnings of the past three years, and no more than 1.5 times book value, or a product of the two no higher than 22.5. (Book, 1973)
  • Strict on purpose. Few companies pass all seven. The tests are meant to keep a defensive investor away from trouble, not to find the next great growth story. (Book, 1973)

Ask yourself

  • Try the tests on one company you know. Which does it fail?
  • Why might a great company fail these tests?
