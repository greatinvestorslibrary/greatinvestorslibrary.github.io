John Maynard Keynes: His three principles

In May 1938 he summed up successful investment in three principles: careful selection, steadfast holding, balance.

  • 1. Careful selection. A few investments, picked because they are cheap compared with what they are likely to be worth over the next few years, and compared with the alternatives. (Memo, 1938)
  • 2. Steadfast holding. Own them in size and refuse to be shaken out, for years if needed, until they deliver or you can see you were wrong. (Memo, 1938)
  • 3. Balance. Mix your risks even though each holding is big, ideally with some that tend to move the other way, as gold-mining shares often did against other shares. (Memo, 1938)
  • The £1 note. Selling something worth £1 for 15 shillings (75p), hoping to buy it back for 12s 6d (about 62p), is a poor trade. So is refusing to buy it at 15 shillings because it looks too good to be true. (Memo, 1938)
“a steadfast holding of these in fairly large units through thick and thin”John Maynard Keynes, Memo to King's College, 1938

Ask yourself

  • Which of the three principles do you find hardest?
  • What does the £1 note story tell you about selling cheap shares to buy them back cheaper?
