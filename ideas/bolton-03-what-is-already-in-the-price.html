Anthony Bolton · Big idea 3 of 10

Anthony Bolton: What is already in the price

Look for a gap between price and value, measured in several ways, then give it time to close.

  • Hunt for anomalies. He bought where the price looked out of line with what the company was worth, then waited for the gap to close. (Morningstar)
  • Several measures, no favourite. Price to earnings, company value to cash profits, free cash flow yield, price to sales and cash returns on investment, each against history and against other companies. (Morningstar)
  • The right tool for the job. For housebuilders he thought price to earnings nearly useless, because one-off land sales distort profits. Price to adjusted book value worked better. (Morningstar)
  • Spotting beats timing. Seeing a mispricing is easier than knowing when it will correct. Some ideas took one year, some several. (Morningstar)
  • Stand firm through noise. Share prices and profits can drift apart for a year or two. Keep testing the idea rather than giving up on it. (Morningstar)
“easier to spot an anomaly than knowing exactly when it is going to correct”Anthony Bolton, as quoted by Morningstar

Ask yourself

  • Which two measures would you use to value a business you know well?
  • How long are you prepared to wait for a cheap share to be noticed?
