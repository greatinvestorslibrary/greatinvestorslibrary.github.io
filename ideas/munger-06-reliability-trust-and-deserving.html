Charlie Munger: Reliability, trust and deserving

He thought most of what people want in life is earned by being someone others can rely on. Trust that is deserved is the strongest arrangement there is.

  • Be reliable. First on his own list of routes to misery was being unreliable: not faithfully doing what you have promised. He said it can outweigh all your other virtues combined. (HS 1986)
  • Learnable by almost anyone. In a 2006 note he repeated that reliability is essential for progress in life, and that almost anyone can learn it to great advantage. (HS 1986)
  • Deserve what you want. The safest way to try to get what you want is to try to deserve it. (USC Law 2007)
  • Admiration-based love. He thought no love is so right as love grounded in admiration, and that it should extend to the instructive dead. (USC Law 2007)
  • Three rules for a career. Sell only what you would buy yourself. Work only for people you respect and admire. Work only with people you enjoy. (Stanford 1996)
  • A seamless web of deserved trust. In your own life, what you want to maximise is trust that has been earned, all the way round. (USC Law 2007)
  • Choose clients like friends. A rule from his years as a lawyer, and one that applies to partners, managers and investments too. (Almanack)
  • Liking distorts. We overlook the faults of people and things we love. Love admirable people, and still check their work. (PHM 2005)
  • Records over impressions. A face-to-face meeting carries too much weight. Underweigh the impression and overweigh the past record. (PHM 2005)
  • Fairness matters. People react strongly when they are treated unfairly. Arrangements that feel unfair breed resentment that outlasts the gain. (PHM 2005)
“Don't sell anything you wouldn't buy yourself.”Charlie Munger, Stanford 1996

Ask yourself

  • Who relies on you, and in what ways do you sometimes let them down?
  • Of the people you trust with money, which have earned it by their record, and which by a good first meeting?
