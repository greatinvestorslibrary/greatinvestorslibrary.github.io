Li Lu: Own a slice of the world

Real wealth is your share of the world's buying power. Over two centuries, owning businesses beat cash, gold and bonds by a vast margin.

  • Fish where the fish are. From Munger he takes the rule to look where others are not: less crowded markets, industries and countries. (Talk, 2026)
  • Wealth is a share of the world. He measures wealth as your percentage of global buying power, not gold, cash or any currency, and keeps it by owning great businesses in growing economies. (Talk, 2026)
  • Two centuries of returns. He cites US figures from 1802: a dollar in cash lost about 95% of its value, gold roughly tripled after inflation, and shares grew about a million times, roughly 6.7% a year after inflation. (Lecture, 2015)
  • Civilisation 3.0. In his book he describes history in stages: hunting and gathering, farming, then free markets plus modern science, the stage in which businesses can compound. (Book, 2020 · Lecture, 2015)
  • China too. In 2015 he argued value investing could work in China: its markets had risen about fifteenfold from 1991 to 2014, while short-term trading still dominated. (Lecture, 2015)

Ask yourself

  • What share of your wealth sits in cash or gold? What has inflation done to it?
  • Which market or industry near you do most investors ignore?
