John Bogle: The simple arithmetic

All investors together own the market. So, before costs, they earn the market's return; after costs, they must earn less. Beating the market is a loser's game for most.

  • Before and after costs. As a group, investors earn exactly the market's return before costs, and less after them. Someone's gain over the average is someone else's loss. (Book, 2007)
  • A loser's game. Trying to beat the market is a loser's game for most, he wrote: the more the managers and brokers take, the less investors make. (Book, 2007)
  • Buffett's bet. Buffett bet that an S&P 500 index fund would beat five funds of hedge funds over ten years. By 2016, $1 million in the index fund had gained $854,000; the hedge funds, $220,000. (Buffett, 2017)
  • Buffett agrees. Buffett told both large and small investors to stick with low-cost index funds. (Buffett, 2017)

Ask yourself

  • Why can't most investors beat the average they make up?
  • Who is on the other side of your trades?
