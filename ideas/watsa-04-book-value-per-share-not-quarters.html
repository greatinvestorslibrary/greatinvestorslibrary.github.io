Prem Watsa · Big idea 4 of 10

Prem Watsa: Book value per share, not quarters

He judges Fairfax by one long-term number: the growth of book value per share. The target has been 15% a year since the start.

  • One number. The goal is to compound book value per share by 15% a year over the long term. (Principles)
  • Not quarterly profit. The focus is long-term growth in book value, not quarterly earnings. (Principles)
  • The record. Since 1985 book value per share has grown 18.7% a year with dividends, and the share price 19.5%. (Letter, 2025)
  • The yardstick. Over the same forty years the S&P 500 returned 11.5% a year with dividends. (Letter, 2025)

Ask yourself

  • Which one number would you judge your own investing by?
  • How often do you look at it?
