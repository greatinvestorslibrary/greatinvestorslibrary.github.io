Charlie Munger: Investing: few, great, and then sit

The big money comes from a handful of great businesses, bought when the odds are clearly favourable, then held for a long time.

  • The great businesses did it. He said the great bulk of their money came from the great businesses. (USC 1994)
  • Bet heavily when the odds are clear. The wise ones bet heavily when the world offers them the opportunity. The rest of the time, they wait. (USC 1994)
  • Twenty punches. Imagine a ticket with only 20 slots for every investment you will ever make. You would think hard and bet big on each one. (USC 1994)
  • Quality compounds. Over long periods a share earns roughly what the business earns. A business earning 18% on capital for 20 or 30 years gives a fine result even bought at an expensive-looking price. (USC 1994)
  • Taxes favour sitting. The way income tax works alone gives a large edge to holding for a long time. (USC 1994)
  • The industry adds nothing, net. The investment management business as a whole adds no value for all its clients combined. A select few managers can. (USC 1994)
  • Opportunity cost. Judge every use of money against the next-best use available. (Almanack)
  • Resist the urge to act. Patience means resisting the natural human bias to do something. (Almanack)
  • Concentration can be rational. Someone with nearly all their wealth held long-term in three fine companies is securely rich. Heavy concentration can be a rational choice. (Foundations 1998)
  • Lumpy results are the price. In super-cat reinsurance, huge swings in annual results, including some very unpleasant years, are inevitable. Judge it over many years. (Wesco 1996)
  • Need less. In a 2006 note he worried about institutions imitating Harvard and Yale's investing out of envy, and favoured reducing one's perceived needs over taking more risk. (USC 1994)
  • Omissions cost most. Among Berkshire's mistakes he named the failure to buy Walmart early. (BRK 2014)
“I think the orthodox view is grossly mistaken.”Charlie Munger, Foundations 1998, on the view that wide diversification is a must
“super-cat reinsurance is not for the faint of heart”Charlie Munger, Wesco 1996

Ask yourself

  • If you had only twenty slots for the rest of your life, which current holdings would earn one?
  • Where have you diversified into ideas you do not believe in strongly?
