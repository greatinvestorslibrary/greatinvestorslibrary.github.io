Seth Klarman: Value it three ways

No single method gives the true value. He discusses several, from future cash flows to break-up value to what similar companies fetch, and urges conservatism.

  • Several methods. The book discusses valuing a business by the present value of its future cash, by its liquidation or break-up value, and by stock market comparisons. (Book, 1991)
  • Conservative always. Since no one can predict when values will rise or fall, value conservatively, giving real weight to worst-case liquidation value. (Summary)
  • A range, not a number. Value is better thought of as a range than a precise figure, so leave room for error. (Book, 1991 · Summary)

Ask yourself

  • What would a company you own fetch if it were broken up and sold?
  • How different are your three estimates?
