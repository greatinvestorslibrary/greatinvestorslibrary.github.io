Anthony Bolton: Meet the managers

He learnt most by meeting the people who run companies, again and again, and writing everything down.

  • Two or three a day. He met two or three companies every working day and valued hearing from managers directly. (Article, 2007)
  • Keep the notes. He handwrote notes in every meeting and kept them all from the late 1980s onwards. (Article, 2007)
  • Candour counts. He looked for managers who were candid and consistent. Like Buffett, he believed a boss who misleads shareholders may end up misleading himself. (Article, 2007)
  • The second meeting matters. Repeated follow-up meetings, not first impressions, uncovered the occasional gem. (MoneyWeek)
  • Owners have a voice. In the 2003 Carlton–Granada merger he pressed for an independent chairman. The press nicknamed him the Quiet Assassin, a name he disliked. (Morningstar)
“I value very highly hearing directly from management”Anthony Bolton, The Spectator, 2007

Ask yourself

  • What would you most want to ask the boss of a company you own?
  • How would you tell whether a manager is being candid?
