John Templeton: Search the whole world

He looked for bargains in every country, not just at home. That was rare in the 1950s, and it gave him far more choices.

  • A global fund. He launched the Templeton Growth Fund in 1954, incorporated in Canada, and it searched for bargains across the world. (Obituary, 2008)
  • Japan. He bought large holdings in Japan while its economy was still recovering from the war, and sold them in the boom years of the 1980s. (interactive investor)
  • More places, more bargains. Diversify by country as well as industry: more markets mean more chances to find something cheap. (Rules, 1993)
  • Travel first. After Oxford he spent seven months travelling the world with a friend, on very little money, before starting work in New York. (Irish Times, 2008)

Ask yourself

  • Which markets do you never look at? Why?
  • What could you learn from investors in another country?
