Charlie Munger: Objectivity and the edge of what you know

Knowing the limits of your competence is worth more than its size. Self-regard and strong ideology are the usual ways people step over the edge without noticing.

  • Circle of competence. Everyone has a circle of competence. Play where you have an edge, and stay out of games where others clearly have it. (USC 1994)
  • Know what you don't know. Know what you know and what you do not. When you lack competence, say so. (Stanford 1996)
  • Use your advantages. Work out where your talents lie and use them. Berkshire avoided high technology partly because it had no edge there. (Stanford 1996)
  • Change your mind. He said he and Buffett were very good at changing their prior conclusions. (Stanford 1996)
  • Earn the right to an opinion. He did not feel entitled to an opinion until he could state the arguments against it better than its supporters could. (USC Law 2007)
  • Self-serving bias. It is in everyone. Use routines and checklists to keep yourself objective. (USC Law 2007)
  • Don't fool yourself. He quoted Feynman: the first principle is not to fool yourself, and you are the easiest person to fool. (Foundations 1998)
  • Don't rush to decide. Uncertainty is uncomfortable, so the mind grabs an answer early. Slow down before a big decision. (PHM 2005)
  • Overoptimism. People expect better outcomes than the odds support. The antidote is habitual use of simple probability. (PHM 2005)
  • Vivid is not important. What comes to mind easily gets too much weight. Look for the evidence that is dull but large. (PHM 2005)
  • Crowds and authority. People copy others and defer to authority, most of all when confused or rushed. Those are the moments to slow down. (PHM 2005)
“The answer is I have a black belt in chutzpah.”Charlie Munger, UCSB 2003, explaining why, with no economics training, he would lecture economists

Ask yourself

  • Which of your current views could you not argue against convincingly? Hold it more loosely.
  • Where is the edge of your circle? Name one thing you own that sits close to it.
