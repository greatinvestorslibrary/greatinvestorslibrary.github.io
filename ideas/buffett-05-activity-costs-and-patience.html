Warren Buffett: Activity, costs and patience

The return from owning good businesses is earned by sitting. Almost everything an investor does beyond that subtracts from it.

  • Newton's fourth law. Newton gave physics three laws of motion and then lost a fortune in the South Sea Bubble. Buffett supplies the missing law: for investors as a group, the more they move, the less they keep. (2005)
  • The Gotrocks family. Imagine one family owns every business in the country. Its wealth grows by what the businesses earn. Then helpers arrive: brokers to trade between cousins, managers to choose the brokers, consultants to choose the managers, and finally hedge funds with a share of profits. Each layer is paid out of the same earnings. He guessed these frictional costs might reach a fifth of all corporate profit. (2005)
  • Three ways investors beat themselves. High costs from trading and fees. Decisions made on tips and fashion in place of valuation. And stop-start timing: getting in after a long rise and out after a long fall. (2004)
  • Wait for the fat pitch. Ted Williams cut the strike zone into cells and swung only at balls in his best one. An investor has it easier, because there are no called strikes. Standing with the bat on your shoulder costs nothing. (1997)
  • Doing nothing is a decision. When you own pieces of excellent businesses, inactivity is the intelligent course. Nobody sells a wonderful subsidiary because of a rate forecast, so why sell a minority stake in one? (1996)
  • The movies would have paid better. After selling McDonald's and trimming other holdings, he told shareholders they would have done better had he spent market hours at the cinema. (1998)
  • Lumpy beats smooth. He takes the higher, bumpier return over the lower, steadier one. Most managers choose the reverse because smooth is easier to explain. (1995 · 1998)
  • A dozen decisions. Looking back over 58 years he counted about twelve really good decisions, roughly one every five years. The record rests on those, plus avoiding ruin in between. (2022)
  • The ten-year bet. A plain index fund against five funds-of-funds chosen by professionals, over 2008 to 2017. The index fund won by a wide margin (chart below). The managers were not stupid. Layers of fees were the difference. (2016 · 2017)
  • One per cent is not small. On his 77-year example, an annual fee of 1% would have cut the ending sum in half. (2018)
  • Idle cash is the lesser evil. Cash earning little is uncomfortable. A purchase made to relieve the discomfort is worse. (1998 · 2003)
“For investors as a whole, returns decrease as motion increases.”Warren Buffett, 2005 letter
“don't even think about owning it for ten minutes”Warren Buffett, 1996 letter
“Performance comes, performance goes. Fees never falter.”Warren Buffett, 2017 letter
“we prefer a lumpy 15% return to a smooth 12%”Warren Buffett, 1998 letter

Ask yourself

  • Add up last year's brokerage, impact cost and short-term tax. What share of my gain did motion consume?
  • How many decisions in my whole career produced most of the result? What was I doing in the months around them?
