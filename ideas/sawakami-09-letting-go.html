Atsuto Sawakami: Letting go

He hired a former part-time actor, handed him the fund, and promised to judge him only over years, even when investors left and the style changed.

  • An unusual hire. In 2007 a 28-year-old part-time actor came to his seminar; he later gave him a job and watched him grow. (Bloomberg, 2016)
  • The handover. In 2013 the young man became chief investment officer and Sawakami's son became president. (Bloomberg, 2016)
  • A hard start. Investors withdrew ¥126.5 billion over three years after the handover. (Bloomberg, 2016)
  • Years, not months. He said he would not judge his successor until five to ten years into the job. (Bloomberg, 2016)
“Children have to burn their fingers to learn.”Atsuto Sawakami, On his successor, Bloomberg, 2016

Ask yourself

  • Who will run your money when you can't?
  • How long would you give a new manager?
