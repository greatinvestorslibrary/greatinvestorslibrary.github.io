John Bogle: Costs matter

Returns are uncertain; costs are certain. Every fee and commission comes straight out of what you earn, and over decades the damage compounds.

  • Two forces. He set the magic of compounding returns against the tyranny of compounding costs. Over long periods costs can eat a large part of your gains. (Book, 2007)
  • You get what you don't pay for. In most things you get what you pay for; in fund investing, he argued, the money you don't pay in fees is what you keep. (Book, 2007)
  • Pick the cheapest. One chapter tells investors simply to focus on the lowest-cost funds. (Book, 2007)
  • A lifelong fight. His obituaries describe a career spent pushing for lower fees for ordinary investors. (AP, 2019)

Ask yourself

  • What do you pay each year, in total, to invest?
  • How much would that cost add up to over 30 years?
