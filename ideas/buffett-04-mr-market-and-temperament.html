Warren Buffett: Mr. Market and temperament

Prices swing far more than values do. The swings are there to be used or ignored. They are never instructions.

  • The hamburger quiz. If you will be eating burgers for life and do not raise cattle, you should want beef to get cheaper. If you will be a net buyer of shares for years, the same logic applies. Only someone about to sell gains from high prices. (1997)
  • If you must time, time against the crowd. His advice to those who insist on timing: be cautious when others are eager, and eager only when others are afraid. (2004)
  • Two kinds of fear. Fear across the market helps you, because it produces bargains. Fear inside you hurts you. An investor who keeps costs low and sits with large, soundly financed businesses will almost certainly do well. (2016)
  • Berkshire itself has halved, more than once. He printed his own stock's four worst falls (chart below) as the case against borrowing to own shares. Nobody can say how far a price may drop in the short run, and debt forces a decision at the worst moment. Without debt, your mind stays clear. (2017)
  • Cinderella at the ball. In a boom everyone plans to leave just before midnight. The trouble is that nobody in the ballroom can see a clock. (2000)
  • Bubbles move wealth; they do not create it. A company that loses money over its lifetime has destroyed value, whatever its market value was at the peak. The gains of promoters came out of the pockets of late buyers. (2000)
  • Being out is the bigger risk. The danger of sitting out of the game is far larger than the danger of staying in it. He does not step in and out on forecasts. (2012)
  • Bad news is the buyer's friend. Pessimism lowers prices. Large opportunities are rare, and when one arrives he acts in size. (2008 · 2009)
  • Ignore the chatter. He spends no time on forecasts of the economy or the market, his own or anyone else's. Beyond the wasted hours, they crowd out the handful of facts about a business that decide the result. (2013)
  • Expect to trail in a boom. A quality-first approach lags when markets roar and leads when they are flat or falling. Know this ahead of time so a strong year for others does not push you into changing method. (2012)
“be fearful when others are greedy and greedy only when others are fearful”Warren Buffett, 2004 letter
“speculation is most dangerous when it looks easiest”Warren Buffett, 2000 letter
“We do better when the wind is in our face.”Warren Buffett, 2012 letter
“When it's raining gold, reach for a bucket, not a thimble.”Warren Buffett, 2009 letter

Ask yourself

  • If my largest holding fell 50% next month with no change in the business, what exactly would I do? Write it down now.
  • Which of my recent trades were responses to price, and which to a change in the business?
