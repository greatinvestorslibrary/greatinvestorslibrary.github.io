Terry Smith: Read the accounts

His first fame came from showing how companies dressed up their profits. Reported profit can mislead; cash is harder to fake.

  • Profits that weren't. In 1990 several big British companies went bust while reporting healthy profits. Their real problem was cash. (Book, 1992)
  • The book that cost his job. In 1992 he expanded his research into a book. His employer, UBS Phillips & Drew, asked him to withdraw it; he refused and was sacked. (Book, 1992)
  • The usual tricks. Acquisition accounting, deferred payments, exceptional items, off-balance-sheet debt, hidden liabilities, brand values, changed depreciation, capitalised costs and currency mismatches. (Book, 1992)
  • It changed the rules. Many of the practices he exposed were later curbed or stopped. (Book, 1992)
  • Habits stick. Managers who flatter their earnings rarely change, so treat it as a lasting warning sign. (Book, 1992)

Ask yourself

  • Where in a company's accounts would you look to check that profits turn into cash?
  • Why might managers want profits to look smoother than they are?
