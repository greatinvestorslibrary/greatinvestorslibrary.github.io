Warren Buffett: Circle of competence

You do not need to understand many businesses. You need to know exactly which ones you do understand, and stay there.

  • Know the perimeter. The size of the circle matters little. Knowing where it ends matters a great deal. You only ever have to value the companies you choose to value. (1996)
  • Why he skipped technology. He did not think the industry was bad. He could not tell which companies would hold a durable advantage ten years out. In 1999 that discipline cost him his worst year against the index, and he gave his own stock-picking a D. He kept the rule. (1999)
  • Exciting is not the same as knowable. The first item on his list of things Berkshire does not do: buy into businesses whose future he cannot assess, however thrilling the product. (2009)
  • Multiply the odds. A plan that needs several separate things to go right has a low chance of success even if each step looks likely. He learned it from a zinc-recovery project. Simple propositions survive contact with reality better. (2004)
  • Passing is free. If you cannot make a rough estimate of earnings five years ahead, move on. Nobody can call you out for not swinging. (1997 · 2013)
  • Know-nothing can beat know-something. An investor who admits to knowing nothing about individual companies and buys a cheap index fund over time will beat most professionals. Trouble starts in between, with knowing a little and acting on a lot. (2013)
  • The circle can grow, slowly. Apple entered the portfolio in 2016 and within five years was one of Berkshire's four giants. He treats it as a consumer franchise with loyal users and large buybacks, which is ground he knows. (2020 · 2021)
  • Keep learning. Among the Munger sayings he passed on: the world changes, and your views must change with it. Staying inside the circle does not mean freezing it. (2022)
“Over time, bad relative numbers will produce unsatisfactory absolute results.”Warren Buffett, 1999 letter

Ask yourself

  • Which holdings sit inside the circle, on its edge, or outside? Does position size follow that order?
  • Where have I mistaken familiarity with a product for understanding of the business?
