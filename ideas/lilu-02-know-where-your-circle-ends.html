Li Lu: Know where your circle ends

Your circle of competence should be small and clearly drawn. The skill is knowing where it ends, and what would prove you wrong.

  • The edge matters most. What counts is not how much you know but knowing exactly where your knowledge stops. (Lecture, 2015 · Talk, 2026)
  • Say when you would be wrong. You should be able to state the conditions under which your view of a company would fail. (Lecture, 2015)
  • Small and hard-won. A real circle is built over years and stays small. Most people cannot see five or ten years ahead for most companies. (Lecture, 2015 · Lecture, 2019)
  • Don't fool yourself. Intellectual honesty comes first: you are the easiest person to fool, and people tend to believe whatever suits them. (Lecture, 2015 · Lecture, 2019)
  • Argue the other side first. From Munger he took the rule of holding no opinion until he can state the opposing case better than its supporters. (Foreword, 2010)

Ask yourself

  • Name one industry where you are truly inside your circle, and one where you only feel you are.
  • What single fact would prove your favourite investment idea wrong?
