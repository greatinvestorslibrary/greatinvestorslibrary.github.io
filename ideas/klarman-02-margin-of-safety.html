Seth Klarman: Margin of safety

Valuation is never precise, the future is unknowable and people make mistakes. So buy only at a price far enough below value to absorb all three.

  • Three reasons. A margin of safety is needed because valuation is imprecise, the future is unpredictable and investors are human. (Summary)
  • Graham's idea. He took the title from Graham, who made the margin of safety the central idea of The Intelligent Investor. (Book, 1991)
  • Haircut your value. Do the work to estimate value, assume you are wrong and cut the estimate, then wait for that lower price. (Summary)
  • Strong in declines. Value investing with a margin of safety has tended to hold up well when markets fall. (Summary)

Ask yourself

  • How big a discount do you insist on before you buy?
  • What mistakes would that discount have to absorb?
