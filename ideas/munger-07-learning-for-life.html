Charlie Munger: Learning for life

He saw lifelong learning as a duty, and as the main reason ordinary people overtake cleverer ones over a working life.

  • Learning machines. He kept seeing people rise who were not always the smartest, but who kept learning. They went to bed each night a little wiser than they were that morning. (USC Law 2007)
  • Wisdom is a moral duty. He held that acquiring wisdom is an obligation, not a hobby. (USC Law 2007)
  • Skills expire. Without lifetime learning you will not do well. Berkshire's skills from one decade would not have carried it through the next. (USC Law 2007)
  • Learn from other people's mistakes. Learning everything from your own experience was on his list of routes to misery. The errors of others are cheaper teachers. (HS 1986)
  • The instructive dead. Admire and learn from the great minds of the past. He counted them among the right objects of love. (USC Law 2007)
  • Keep curiosity alive. Curiosity drives learning and protects against many other errors. (PHM 2005)
  • Use it or lose it. Skills fade without practice. Skills practised to fluency last much longer. (PHM 2005)
  • Learning gets harder with age. Ageing makes new, complex skills harder to acquire. Keep learning and practising, and learn the difficult things while you can. (PHM 2005)
  • Hunt for disconfirming evidence. Darwin trained himself early to weigh seriously any evidence against his own hypotheses. Do the same with your beliefs. (PHM 2005)
  • Maintain what you know. Pilots are made to practise in simulators so skills do not decay. Schedule your own refreshers. (HLS 1998)

Ask yourself

  • What did you learn this month that changed a view you held? If nothing, what does that tell you?
  • Which belief of yours most needs a Darwin list of facts against it?
