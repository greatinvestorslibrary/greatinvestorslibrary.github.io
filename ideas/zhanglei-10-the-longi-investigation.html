Zhang Lei: The LONGi investigation

In 2023 China's securities regulator investigated a Hillhouse unit over undisclosed share sales. Here are the facts, so you can judge for yourself.

  • What happened. HHLR, Hillhouse's public-markets unit, owned 5.85% of the solar company LONGi. In March 2023 it lent out shares, taking its stake below 5%, the level above which sales must be announced. (News, 2023–24)
  • The gap. When the loan ended, its stake was only 4.98%, implying it had sold about 0.9% of the company without an announcement. (News, 2023–24)
  • The probe. In November 2023 the regulator opened an investigation for suspected breaches of share-transfer rules, and the exchanges soon closed the loophole. (News, 2023–24)
  • The response. In April 2024 HHLR began buying the shares back, promising to hand any gain to LONGi. We have found no published final penalty decision. (News, 2023–24)

Ask yourself

  • Why do rules require large shareholders to announce their sales?
  • How would you weigh a firm-level lapse against a long record?
