Qiu Guolu: Cheapness is the hard truth

A low price is the investor's first protection. Even an ordinary company can pay well if bought cheaply enough.

  • Buy cheap to sell cheap. He borrows a retailer's rule, Sam Walton's: you can only sell cheaply if you buy cheaply. In investing, buying cheaply matters even more. (Book, 2014)
  • Ordinary but cheap. An ordinary company bought at a low enough price can still give a fine return. (Book, 2014)
  • Few true greats. He reckoned that among about 2,500 Chinese listed companies perhaps ten were truly great, so most of the time price must do the work. (Book, 2014)
  • Wary of the next great thing. He is suspicious of stories about the next great company, and admits he struggles even to pay a fair price for great ones. (Book, 2014)
“便宜才是硬道理”Qiu Guolu, “Cheapness is the hard truth.” A principle from his 2014 book (our translation)

Ask yourself

  • Can you name a dull company that looks cheap? Why is it cheap?
  • How many truly great companies can you name in your own market?
