Li Lu: A different road from Wall Street

Munger told him his problems were Wall Street's problems. He rebuilt his fund around long-term partners and a sense of duty to their savings.

  • Thanksgiving 2003. He took Munger through every company he owned or followed. Munger said his problems came from the way Wall Street thinks, and offered to invest if he would take a different path. (Foreword, 2010)
  • A new structure. With Munger's help he rebuilt the fund like Buffett's early partnerships: no shorting, long lock-ups for partners who stayed, and no new investors. (Foreword, 2010)
  • Your parents' savings. He says a manager should treat clients' money like the lifetime savings of their own parents, and that people without that instinct should not be in the business. (Lecture, 2015)
  • Fees that favour managers. He criticises the common hedge fund fee of 2% a year plus 20% of profits as serving managers better than clients. (Lecture, 2015)
  • Deserved trust. He recalls Munger's ideal: a seamless web of deserved trust. Keep partners who share your approach, rather than chasing the most money. (Talk, 2026)

Ask yourself

  • If someone managed your parents' savings, what would you want them never to do?
  • Why might a smaller fund with patient partners do better than a bigger one?
