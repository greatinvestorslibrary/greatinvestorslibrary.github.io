His thinking in five minutes

Qiu Guolu formed his approach during six years at Wedge Capital in the United States, ran investment at China Southern Asset Management from 2008, and in 2014 founded Gaoyi Asset Management, a home for several star managers that grew past 100 billion yuan. His bestselling book boils investing down to three questions: why is it cheap, why is it good, and why buy now?

  • 3 questions: why is it cheap, why is it good, why now: his whole method
  • about 10: truly great companies, by his count, among roughly 2,500 then listed in China; so price must do much of the work
  • 6 or 7 in 10: years in which, he reckons, a sound method beats the market; a poor one manages 3 or 4
  • ¥100bn+: managed by Gaoyi, the firm he founded, by early 2021

His thinking, one line each

  • Cheapness is the hard truth. Even an ordinary company can pay well if bought cheaply enough.
  • Pricing power is the core advantage. Do what you can repeat, and what others cannot copy.
  • Win first, then fight. Wait until an industry's battle is over, then back the winner.
  • Take what others discard. Go where the crowd is not, and find out why it left.
  • Count the moons, not the stars. Prefer industries with one or two dominant firms to crowded ones.
  • Process over outcome. A lucky win is not a right bet. Judge a method over five to ten years.

The big ideas

  1. Three questions. All his analysis comes down to three questions: why is it cheap, why is it good, and why buy now?
  2. Cheapness is the hard truth. A low price is the investor's first protection.
  3. Pricing power is the core advantage. A company with a real edge can raise prices without losing customers.
  4. Count the moons, not the stars. Look at the whole industry, not just the company.
  5. Win first, then fight. Crowded, fast-growing industries make poor hunting grounds.
  6. Take what others discard. Go where the crowd is not, and be careful where it is packed.
  7. A lucky win is not a right bet. Judge your method, not this year's result.
  8. Clients first. A fund firm must put compliance before growth, clients before itself, and results before size.
  9. Value investing works in China. He has tried most methods.
  10. Even legends stumble. After two hard years he wrote to investors about his mistakes, and what he would watch more closely.

Three questions, one checklist

Three questions, eight checks

  1. Cheap: low against its own history and its peers, on more than one measure
  2. Good: it does something it can repeat, and others cannot copy
  3. Good: its industry has one or two clear leaders, and it is one of them
  4. Good: its products don't change every two or three years
  5. Good: I know how it makes its profits, and its strategy fits
  6. Now: the crowd is avoiding it, not chasing it
  7. Now: the industry's battle is over, and I am buying the winner
  8. I will judge this decision over five to ten years, not six months

Count the moons: industries from best to worst

  1. One moon. A single dominant firm.
  2. A moon with bright stars. One leader and a few strong rivals.
  3. Two or three moons. A handful of equals.
  4. A sky full of stars. Many small firms fighting on price.

From his 2017 lecture. The hard part is defining the sky: which firms truly compete with each other.

Words to know

A shares
Shares of Chinese companies listed in Shanghai and Shenzhen, traded in yuan.
Price to earnings (P/E)
The share price divided by the profit per share.
Price to book (P/B)
The share price divided by the company's net assets per share.
Pricing power
The ability to raise prices without losing customers.
Porter's five forces
A way to judge an industry: rivals, new entrants, substitutes, and the bargaining power of suppliers and customers.
DuPont analysis
Splitting return on equity into profit margin, asset turnover and borrowing.
Value trap
A share that looks cheap but stays cheap, because the business keeps getting worse.
Contrarian
Deliberately going against the crowd.
Private fund
A fund sold only to qualified investors, with fewer public reporting rules than a public fund.
