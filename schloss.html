His thinking in five minutes

Walter Schloss never went to college. He started as a Wall Street runner at 18, learned from Benjamin Graham at evening classes and worked for him before starting his own partnership in 1955. For 47 years he bought cheap shares by simple rules, owned around a hundred at a time, never visited managements and beat the market by a wide margin. Buffett called him one of the good guys of Wall Street.

  • 21.3% a year: his partnership, 1956 to 1984, before his share (16.1% for his partners), as Buffett reported
  • 47 years: 1956 to 2002, about 15% to 16% a year, by different counts
  • $100,000: the money 19 investors gave him to start in 1955
  • ≈ $11,000: his office costs in a year when his partnership earned $19 million, as Buffett noted

His thinking, one line each

  • Price first. Price, compared with value, is what matters most.
  • Assets over earnings. Assets change slowly; earnings can vanish quickly.
  • Buy near the lows. Look at shares near the bottom of their range of recent years.
  • Own many. Around a hundred shares at a time, bought and sold in steps.
  • Public numbers only. No company visits, no inside talk: just the reports.
  • Calm and patient. Keep fear and greed out, and don't sell too soon.

The big ideas

  1. Price comes first. His first factor: price, compared with value, is the most important thing.
  2. Assets over earnings. He preferred buying assets at a discount to buying earnings.
  3. Buy near the lows. He liked shares trading near the bottom of their range of recent years, and he was patient once he had bought.
  4. Own many, buy in steps. He spread his money across around a hundred shares and bought and sold gradually rather than all at once.
  5. Public numbers only. He never visited managements or chased inside information.
  6. Keep emotions out. Fear and greed are the worst emotions for an investor.
  7. Don't sell too soon. Many investors sell after a quick gain.
  8. Compounding, shares and leverage. Let returns compound, prefer shares to bonds over the long run, and be careful with borrowed money.
  9. Paid only when partners gained. He charged no management fee.
  10. A quiet life on Wall Street. He came in as a teenage runner, learned from Graham, served in the war and ran his partnership for 47 years with his son.

His one-page note

His 16 factors, in our words

  1. Price, compared with value, matters most.
  2. Work out what the business is worth; a share is part of it.
  3. Start from book value, and check the debt.
  4. Be patient: shares don't rise at once.
  5. No tips, no quick moves; don't sell on bad news.
  6. Dare to stand alone, but test your thinking. Buy and sell in steps.
  7. Once decided, have the courage of your convictions.
  8. Have a method and follow it.
  9. Don't rush to sell; re-check value and the market's mood.
  10. Buying near the low of recent years helps.
  11. Prefer buying assets cheaply to buying earnings.
  12. Listen to people you respect; decide for yourself.
  13. Keep fear and greed out of it.
  14. Remember compounding and the rule of 72.
  15. Prefer shares to bonds over the long run.
  16. Be careful with borrowed money.

Our paraphrase of his typed one-page note. Read the original at the link in the sources.

Would it pass a look in his style?

  1. The price is at or below book value
  2. The price is near the low of the past few years
  3. Debt is well below shareholders' equity
  4. The company has a long record, good and bad years included
  5. You can judge it from published reports alone
  6. It would be one of many holdings, not a big bet

Words to know

Book value
A company's assets minus its liabilities, as shown in its accounts.
Runner
A junior clerk who carried papers and messages between Wall Street firms.
Partnership
A pooled investment fund run by a manager for a group of partners.
Leverage
Investing with borrowed money.
Rule of 72
Divide 72 by a yearly return to find roughly how many years money takes to double.
Inside information
Important facts about a company not yet made public; trading on it is illegal.
