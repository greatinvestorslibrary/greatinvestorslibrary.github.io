# Duan Yongping

> Built the companies behind OPPO and vivo, then turned investor: buy businesses you understand, never borrow, and keep a list of things you won't do.

Source page: https://alienspacex.com/duan.html · Section: China · Updated 2026-11-28

## Core idea

Buying a stock is buying the company. A company is worth the cash it will produce in future, discounted. Full stop.

## Summary

Duan Yongping built two electronics companies, Subor and BBK, whose offshoots grew into the phone makers OPPO and vivo. He stepped back in 2001, moved to California and turned to investing: a large bet on NetEase when it was in trouble, then Apple and Kweichow Moutai for many years. His rules: buying a stock is buying the company, buy only what you understand, never borrow, and keep a list of things you will not do.

## Record at a glance

- **about 100×**: his gain on NetEase, bought in its post-2000 crisis, by his own account (not audited)
- **$620,100**: his winning bid for lunch with Buffett in 2006, a record then; the money went to charity
- **1 → 3**: BBK split into three companies in 2001; they grew into OPPO, vivo and imoo
- **0**: interest-bearing loans: a fixed rule at BBK, OPPO and vivo

## Big ideas

### 1. Buying a stock is buying the company

One sentence from Buffett changed how he invested. A share is part of a company, and a company is worth the cash it will produce from now on.

- **The sentence that clicked.** In America he first tried charts and could make no sense of them, until Buffett's line that buying a stock is buying a company made everything clear. (Magazine, 2025)
- **Value is future cash.** A company is worth all the cash it will produce from now until it ends, discounted back to today. (Book, 2025)
- **Fewer than one in a hundred.** The idea sounds simple, he says, but perhaps fewer than 1% of investors truly understand it. (Interview, 2025)
- **The company's money, not the market's.** Investors earn what their companies earn. People forever talking about bull and bear markets are really hoping to win other traders' money. (Xueqiu replies)
- **No forecast needed.** He decides by comparing opportunities, not by predicting the economy. (Interview, 2025)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/duan-01-buying-a-stock-is-buying-the-company.html

### 2. Only what you understand

Be honest about what you understand. He says he truly understands only a few companies, and his losses came when he strayed.

- **Know what you know.** Honesty with yourself is the heart of a circle of competence: know what you know and admit what you don't. (Talk, 2018)
- **A short list.** He says he understands Apple, Tencent and Moutai, only partly understands a few others, and avoids most of the rest. (Interview, 2025)
- **A grey zone.** He admits the line between understanding and not is blurry, which is why he keeps to the few he knows best. (Interview, 2025)
- **Games he knew.** He credits his NetEase success to knowing the games business from his Subor years. (Magazine, 2025 · Interview, 2025)
- **Others may understand what you don't.** Not understanding a company is no judgement on it; it simply isn't for you. (Talk, 2018)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/duan-02-only-what-you-understand.html

### 3. The stop-doing list

Doing the right thing mostly means refusing to do wrong things. Most people know what is wrong; they do it anyway for a short-term gain.

- **Not doing wrong.** Most people can tell right from wrong. They do wrong things anyway because the short-term pull is strong, like smokers who know the harm. (Talk, 2018)
- **Stop early.** When you realise something is a mistake, stop at once. Stopping is cheapest at the start. (Talk, 2018)
- **BBK's list.** No selling on credit, no late payments to suppliers, no late wages, no haggling, no making products for others, no interest-bearing loans, no attacking rivals. (Magazine, 2025 · Talk, 2018)
- **Saying no to Walmart.** He once refused an order for a million VCD players without even discussing price: haggling would only waste everyone's time. (Magazine, 2025)
- **Learned the hard way.** Many items on the list came from painful lessons; each rule saves them from a class of mistakes. (Interview, 2025 · Magazine, 2025)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/duan-03-the-stop-doing-list.html

### 4. Never borrow

His companies take no interest-bearing loans, and he never borrows to invest. Debt makes you faster in both directions.

- **No loans.** BBK, OPPO and vivo take no interest-bearing loans, so they cannot die from running out of cash. He cites Munger: most failures come from borrowing too much. (Talk, 2018)
- **One soaking.** Borrowing speeds up gains and losses alike. Getting your feet wet is unavoidable; getting soaked can be permanent. (Talk, 2018)
- **Crises as chances.** With ample cash, every crisis became a chance to act rather than a threat. (Talk, 2018)
- **Missing out is fine.** Missing some opportunities is acceptable, as long as you catch the right ones. (Talk, 2018)
- **Rivals gone, BBK still here.** Most of his old competitors have disappeared; he believes avoiding debt is a big part of why his companies survived. (Talk, 2018)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/duan-04-never-borrow.html

### 5. Right business, right people, right price

Look at the business model and the culture first. Price comes last.

- **Three things, in order.** Right business, right people, right price, an order he credits to Buffett. Price matters least. (Talk, 2018)
- **Two filters.** He looks at two things, the business model and the corporate culture. If he dislikes either, he stops looking. (Review, 2026 · Interview, 2025)
- **Cheap, seen from ten years out.** Whether a share is expensive today is a short-term question. Look back from ten years ahead. (Talk, 2018)
- **Different, or nothing.** Products no different from rivals' can only compete on price and rarely make money. He calls 'value for money' an excuse for weak products. (Talk, 2018)
- **No cash flow, no investment.** He avoids anything that produces no cash flow, such as Bitcoin. (Talk, 2018)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/duan-05-right-business-right-people-right-price.html

### 6. Benfen and a calm mind

Benfen means doing what you should, even when nobody asked. Add a calm, ordinary mind and, he says, you have the whole secret of BBK.

- **Their only secret.** Asked whether BBK's success came from advertising or incentives, he says no: it came from benfen plus a calm mind. (Talk, 2018 · Review, 2026)
- **Do the right thing, and do it right.** He came across Peter Drucker's line as a student; it became BBK's motto. (Interview, 2025 · Review, 2026)
- **What great bosses share.** He cites a study of top chief executives: their personalities differed in every way except one, integrity. (Talk, 2018)
- **Every word a promise.** Don't deceive customers or investors; what you say, you must do. (Talk, 2018)
- **Trust before money.** He left Subor because its owner broke promises about shares: a problem of trust, not money. (Interview, 2025)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/duan-06-benfen-and-a-calm-mind.html

### 7. Hold great companies

Good companies rarely need selling. He sells when the business changes or something clearly better appears.

- **No need to sell.** By 2018 he had never sold Apple, saying good companies don't need selling. (Talk, 2018)
- **Two reasons to sell.** The company's earning power changes, or a clearly better company he understands appears at a good price. (Talk, 2018 · Interview, 2025)
- **Fully invested.** He calls himself a full-position investor and dislikes idle cash, since bank interest earns less than good companies. (Interview, 2025)
- **Moutai at half price.** When Moutai's shares fell by about half, he held on and kept buying. (Interview, 2025)
- **Trimming, not trading.** By 2025 he thought Apple's shares expensive and was trimming by selling call options. (Interview, 2025)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/duan-07-hold-great-companies.html

### 8. NetEase, Apple and Moutai

Three bets made him famous as an investor. Each fits his rules: a business he understood, a culture he liked, a sensible price.

- **NetEase.** He bought heavily when the shares were in panic and the company held more cash than its market value. They rose about twentyfold in six months and more than a hundredfold in all. (Interview, 2025 · AFP, 2017)
- **Sold too early?** He later sold, saying he felt uneasy holding so much, and admits the later share price suggests he sold too soon. (Talk, 2018)
- **Apple.** He began buying in 2011, seeing the shift to software and the platform, and a culture that puts users first and refuses products that add too little. (Interview, 2025)
- **Moutai.** A unique product whose taste does not change, loyal customers, and steady dividends and buybacks. (Interview, 2025)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/duan-08-netease-apple-and-moutai.html

### 9. The Buffett lunch and giving back

In 2006 he paid a record sum to lunch with Buffett, to charity. He plans to give most of his money away, and gives with conditions that make gifts last.

- **A record bid.** In 2006 he paid $620,100 at Buffett's charity lunch auction, the first winner from mainland China. (SCMP, 2006)
- **Giving most away.** He said he planned to follow Buffett by giving most of his money away. (SCMP, 2006)
- **Gifts that last.** In 2025 and 2026 he gave Moutai shares to education funds, on condition the shares are kept and only the dividends spent. (Review, 2026)
- **Three wishes for graduates.** At Zhejiang University in 2011 he told graduates: have no grand ambitions, keep a list of things you won't do, and be upright. (Review, 2026)
- **Improve yourself.** Asked about beating Apple, he said they think about improving themselves, not about surpassing anyone. (AFP, 2017)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/duan-09-the-buffett-lunch-and-giving-back.html

### 10. The mistakes he admits

He is frank about his errors. Most came when he speculated or strayed outside what he understood.

- **Baidu.** A speculative bet on Baidu ended in a short squeeze that cost him $100 to 200 million. He calls it speculation, not investing. (Talk, 2018)
- **General Electric.** He bought GE in 2008, sold a few years later, and says he never really grasped its business model. (Interview, 2025)
- **Oil.** About $100 million put into an oil index lost more than $10 million before he sold. (Interview, 2025)
- **Smartphones.** BBK was slow to see the shift to smartphones; losses in 2012 and 2013 drained much of its cash. (Interview, 2025)
- **Speculation versus investing.** He says his real investing mistakes were few and small; the big losses came from speculation. (Talk, 2018)

## Special section

**Your own stop-doing list**

- I never borrow money to buy shares
- I never bet against a company by short selling
- I never buy a business I can't explain
- I never buy on a tip or a story alone
- I never trade to win other traders' money
- I never keep a mistake going just because stopping hurts
- I never buy a company whose culture I dislike
- I never pay for something that produces no cash

**BBK's stop-doing list, as reported**

- No selling on credit.
- No late payments to suppliers.
- No late wages.
- No haggling: one price for every customer.
- No making products for other brands.
- No interest-bearing loans.
- No attacking competitors.

His decision test, in his own description: first ask whether something is the right thing to do. Only then ask whether it makes money.

## Words to know

- **Benfen (本分)**: Doing what you should, honestly and without being asked; not taking advantage of others.
- **A calm, ordinary mind (平常心)**: Staying rational and returning to fundamentals whatever is happening around you.
- **Stop-doing list (不为清单)**: A written list of things a person or company will not do, whatever the short-term gain.
- **Discounted cash flow**: Valuing a company by adding up the cash it will produce in future, each year's cash shrunk back to today's money.
- **Short squeeze**: When a share that many have bet against rises fast, forcing them to buy back at high prices.
- **Call option**: A contract giving someone the right to buy a share at a set price. Selling one brings in cash now, but caps your gain.
- **Contract manufacturing (OEM)**: Making products for another company to sell under its brand.
- **Opportunity cost**: What you give up by choosing one use of money over the next best one.
