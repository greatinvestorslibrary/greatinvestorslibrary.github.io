# Luiz Barsi Filho

> Shined shoes in a São Paulo tenement, then built his own pension one dividend share at a time, and became Brazil's biggest individual investor.

Source page: https://alienspacex.com/barsi.html · Section: the wider world · Updated 2026-11-28

## Core idea

A private pension. Build your own retirement out of dividend-paying shares.

## Summary

Luiz Barsi Filho lost his father at one and grew up in a São Paulo tenement, shining shoes and selling sweets. At 14 he found work at a stockbroker. Worried that Brazil's state pension would fail him, he began in the early 1970s to buy shares in one durable company a month and reinvest every dividend. Half a century later he is Brazil's biggest individual investor, said to receive more than a million reais a day in dividends, and he still rides the metro.

## Record at a glance

- **14**: his age when he started work at a stockbroker, after years shining shoes
- **~10 years**: until his dividends passed what a state pension would pay him
- **R$0.50**: his cost for Santander Brasil preferred shares later worth over R$25
- **R$1m+**: a day in dividends, as reported by Fast Company Brasil, from a portfolio near US$2 billion

## Big ideas

### 1. A tenement childhood

He lost his father at one, grew up with his mother in a São Paulo tenement, and worked from about seven.

- **Brás.** He was born in São Paulo in 1939 and raised by his mother in a tenement in the Brás district. (InfoMoney · Book, 2022)
- **Fatherless at one.** His father, an immigrant, died when he was a year old. (InfoMoney)
- **Small jobs.** From about seven he shined shoes, sold sweets at a cinema and helped his mother as a tailor's apprentice. (InfoMoney)
- **Never again.** His stated aim was never to be poor again. (InfoMoney)

### 2. A stockbroker at fourteen

At 14 he got a job at a stockbroker and fell for company accounts. He went on to study accounting, law and economics.

- **The broker.** At 14 he started at a stockbroker, where he became fascinated by balance sheets. (InfoMoney)
- **Many degrees.** He trained as an accounting technician, then studied law and economics. (InfoMoney · Investidor10)
- **Many jobs.** He taught balance-sheet analysis, worked as an auditor and analyst, and was a newspaper's economics editor for 18 years. (InfoMoney · Investidor10)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/barsi-02-a-stockbroker-at-fourteen.html

### 3. Shares guarantee the future

Before 30 he feared Brazil's state pension would fail him. In 1970 he wrote a study of which sectors last longest, and built his own pension from their shares.

- **A worry.** He concluded that Brazil's social security system could not be relied on. (InfoMoney)
- **The study.** In 1970 he ranked sectors by how long they would last: food, sanitation, energy, mining and finance. (InfoMoney · Investidor10)
- **A name that stuck.** The study's title, Shares Guarantee the Future, now names an education company run by his daughter. (Investidor10 · Fast Company)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/barsi-03-shares-guarantee-the-future.html

### 4. One thousand shares a month

He chose São Paulo's power company and bought about a thousand shares every month until he reached his target.

- **A choice.** His first pick, a food company, was dropped because of succession worries; he chose the power company CESP instead. (InfoMoney)
- **A target.** He aimed for 100,000 shares and bought about 1,000 a month. (InfoMoney)
- **Salary, then dividends.** He bought from his salary and reinvested the dividends. (Investidor10)
- **A decade.** Within about ten years his dividends exceeded a state pension. (InfoMoney)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/barsi-04-one-thousand-shares-a-month.html

### 5. The pension portfolio

His method: own durable companies that pay good dividends, bought cheaply, held for decades.

- **Perennial.** He looks for companies in durable sectors that pay good dividends and trade below book value. (InfoMoney)
- **Quality tests.** He judges product longevity, governance, management, finances, profits and how regularly dividends come. (InfoMoney)
- **A shareholder's mind.** He saw himself as owning part of profitable businesses, not trading in and out. (Investidor10)
- **Decades.** Several of his holdings have been owned for more than 30 years. (InfoMoney)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/barsi-05-the-pension-portfolio.html

### 6. Banks, energy, sanitation, insurance, telecoms

His followers sum up where he starts looking with one word: BESST, the sectors whose services people always need.

- **BESST.** Banks, energy, sanitation, insurance and telecoms are the starting filter taught by his followers. (Investidor10)
- **Only a start.** The sector is where the analysis begins, not where it ends. (Investidor10)
- **What he avoids.** He has excluded the state oil company, airlines, tourism and appliance retail. (InfoMoney)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/barsi-06-banks-energy-sanitation-insurance-telecoms.html

### 7. What he never does

He never invests money he may need, never sells because he has to, and avoids leverage, options and futures.

- **Spare money only.** Never invest money you might need. (InfoMoney)
- **Never forced.** Never sell out of necessity. (InfoMoney)
- **No gambling tools.** He avoids leverage, futures and options. (InfoMoney)
- **Fixed loss.** He jokes that fixed income, renda fixa, is really perda fixa: a fixed loss. (InfoMoney)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/barsi-07-what-he-never-does.html

### 8. Patience pays

His best results came from holding for decades: shares bought for cents grew into fortunes and paid him every year.

- **Santander.** He bought Santander Brasil preferred shares for about R$0.50; in 2020 they traded above R$25. (InfoMoney)
- **One holding's income.** In 2019 one power company alone paid him about R$4 million in dividends. (InfoMoney)
- **A million a day.** His portfolio, near US$2 billion, is reported to pay him over R$1 million a day. (Fast Company)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/barsi-08-patience-pays.html

### 9. Mistakes and a clean verdict

He lost money in banks that failed in the 1990s. In 2024 Brazil's regulator unanimously cleared him of an insider-trading charge.

- **Failed banks.** He counts investments in banks that collapsed, including Banco Nacional, among his mistakes. (InfoMoney · Investidor10)
- **The charge.** The regulator examined Unipar shares he bought in May 2021 while on its board. (CVM, 2024)
- **The verdict.** In March 2024 it acquitted him unanimously. (CVM, 2024 · InfoMoney, 2024)
- **His defence.** He said the purchase was made to collect dividends, in line with his strategy. (InfoMoney, 2024)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/barsi-09-mistakes-and-a-clean-verdict.html

### 10. Rich, but simple

He still lives simply, rides the metro on a free senior pass, and asks what wealth means.

- **The metro.** He commutes by metro with a free pass for older people. (InfoMoney)
- **What is rich?** Asked if he is rich, he turns the question back: what does wealth mean to you? (Book, 2022)
- **A family method.** Two of his children work in investing, and one teaches his method. (Fast Company)
- **Still on boards.** In 2026 he became vice-chairman of Unipar's board. (Investidor10)

## Special section

**Would it fit his pension portfolio?**

- It is in a durable, essential sector: banks, energy, sanitation, insurance or telecoms
- It has paid dividends regularly for years
- Its products will still be needed in decades
- It is well governed and well managed
- Its finances are sound and it makes a profit
- The price is reasonable, ideally near or below book value
- You are buying with money you will not need

**His three nevers**

- Never invest money you may need.
- Never sell because you have to.
- Never use leverage, futures or options.

**An honest note.** Brazil's securities regulator, the CVM, brought a case alleging that Luiz Barsi Filho traded on inside information when he bought Unipar shares worth about R$1.1 million on 28 May 2021, while he sat on the company's board, days before it announced a deal. On 26 March 2024 the CVM's board acquitted him unanimously. His defence was that the purchase was made to collect dividends, in line with his long-standing strategy. He has also lost money on banks that failed in the 1990s. We include this because the library puts integrity first and tells readers what is on the public record.

## Words to know

- **Dividend**: A share of a company's profit paid to its shareholders.
- **Dividend yield**: Yearly dividends as a percentage of the share price.
- **Book value**: Assets minus liabilities, as shown in the accounts.
- **Preferred shares (PN)**: In Brazil, shares with priority on dividends but usually no vote.
- **INSS**: Brazil's state social security and pension system.
- **Renda fixa**: Fixed income: bonds and deposits. He calls it perda fixa, fixed loss.
- **CVM**: Comissão de Valores Mobiliários, Brazil's securities regulator.
