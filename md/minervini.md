# Mark Minervini

> Lost all his capital in the 1980s, then studied history's biggest winning stocks and won the U.S. Investing Championship twice, in 1997 and 2021.

Source page: https://alienspacex.com/minervini.html · Section: the traders · Updated 2026-11-28

**For adults (18+). Trading is risky.** This page is about a professional trader who buys and sells fast-moving shares, often in large positions, and sells quickly when they fall. It is for education, not a trading course or a recommendation. Most individuals who trade lose money. In India, SEBI found that 93% of individual equity F&O traders lost money between FY22 and FY24. Contest wins are single years. Past results, his or anyone's, do not predict future ones. Never trade with money you cannot afford to lose.

## Core idea

Study the winners. Copy what the biggest winners looked like before they ran.

## Summary

Mark Minervini left school early, played music, bought his first stock in 1983, and lost money for about six years until his capital was gone. Then he studied the biggest winning stocks in history, built a method he calls SEPA, and won the U.S. Investing Championship twice, in 1997 and 2021. His trademark is a strict checklist: buy only leading stocks in strong uptrends, at a precise point, and cut every loss small.

## Big ideas

### 1. A musician who lost it all

He wanted to be a musician, bought his first stock in 1983, and made money at once. Then he lost money for about six years, until his capital was gone.

- **The studio.** He sold his music studio and used the money to trade. (Elearnmarkets)
- **Easy money.** His first stock, Allis-Chalmers, bought at $4 near its yearly low, made a quick profit. (IBD)
- **Six bad years.** He says he made every mistake possible and lost money for almost six years. (MarketWatch)
- **Wiped out.** By the end of the 1980s all his trading capital was gone. (IBD)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/minervini-01-a-musician-who-lost-it-all.html

### 2. Study the winners

Instead of guessing, he studied what the biggest winning stocks in history looked like before their big moves, and built a 'leadership profile' to copy.

- **The books.** Richard Love's Superperformance Stocks and William Jiler's work became the backbone of his method. (Stockopedia)
- **Weinstein.** After meeting Stan Weinstein in 1990 he added trend analysis to his work. (Stockopedia)
- **The profile.** He reversed his old habits and copied what winning stocks looked like before they ran. (MarketWatch)
- **Two flaws.** Looking back, he found he let small losses grow and had no clear rules for buying. (IBD)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/minervini-02-study-the-winners.html

### 3. SEPA: a specific entry point

In 1993 he founded Quantech Research Group, selling research based on his method, Specific Entry Point Analysis, or SEPA.

- **The firm.** Quantech Research Group, founded in 1993, sold his research to professional investors. (TWST, 1998)
- **The name.** He called the method Specific Entry Point Analysis: SEPA for short. (TWST, 1998)
- **Both sides.** SEPA combines a stock's price trend with its earnings growth. (Stockopedia · Goodreads)
- **A fund.** In 2000 he started a hedge fund, also called Quantech. (7 Circles)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/minervini-03-sepa-a-specific-entry-point.html

### 4. Stage 2 only

Borrowing Stan Weinstein's idea that stocks move through four stages, he buys only in Stage 2: when a stock is already in a strong, rising trend.

- **Four stages.** Resting, rising, topping, falling: he wants only the rising stage. (Stockopedia · ChartMill)
- **No bottom-fishing.** He avoids shares that have fallen far from their highs, however cheap they look. (ChartMill)
- **Strength first.** A stock must prove itself by rising before he will look at it. (ChartMill · MarketWatch)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/minervini-04-stage-2-only.html

### 5. The Trend Template

His trademark filter: eight tests on price, moving averages and the yearly high and low. A stock that fails any of them is not a candidate.

- **The averages.** Price above its 50-, 150- and 200-day averages, stacked in that order, with the 200-day rising. (ChartMill)
- **Near the high.** Within 25% of its 52-week high, and well above its 52-week low. (ChartMill · MQL5)
- **Two versions.** His first book asks for 30% above the low; his second says 25%. (MQL5)
- **Relative strength.** It should be beating at least 70% of other stocks. (ChartMill)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/minervini-05-the-trend-template.html

### 6. The VCP

His favourite setup is the Volatility Contraction Pattern: a rising stock pauses, and each pullback is smaller than the one before, until the price breaks out.

- **Shrinking swings.** Pullbacks of, say, 25%, then 15%, then 8%, each one tighter. (ChartMill)
- **Quiet volume.** Trading volume dries up as the pattern tightens: sellers are running out. (ChartMill)
- **The pivot.** He buys as the price breaks above the final tight range, with a stop just below it. (ChartMill)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/minervini-06-the-vcp.html

### 7. Cut losses small

After losing everything once, he made one rule above all: cut losses while they are small. He says no loss should ever reach 10%, and the average should be far smaller.

- **Nicked to death.** If the market is to beat him, he says, it must do it one small loss at a time. (IBD)
- **10% at most.** He sets a firm maximum loss of 10%, with an average loss much lower. (Financial Wisdom)
- **Staggered stops.** He may split a position, with half stopped out at 6% and half at 10%. (Stockopedia)
- **Half right.** He expects to be right only about half the time, so losses must stay smaller than gains. (Stockopedia)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/minervini-07-cut-losses-small.html

### 8. 220% a year

Jack Schwager reported that from mid-1994 to 1999 he averaged about 220% a year, with only one small losing quarter. These are his own figures, not an audited fund.

- **The run.** About 220% a year compounded over five and a half years from mid-1994. (TradingMarkets)
- **Worst year.** His worst year in that stretch was a gain of 128%. (7 Circles)
- **One down quarter.** Only one quarter lost money, by less than 1%. (TradingMarkets)
- **The total.** His books describe this as five years of triple-digit returns, about 33,500% in total. (Goodreads)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/minervini-08-220-a-year.html

### 9. Two championships

To prove his method with real money, he entered the U.S. Investing Championship twice and won both times: +155% in 1997 and a record +334.8% in 2021.

- **1997.** He won with a 155% return, trading only shares. (PR Newswire, 2022)
- **2021.** He won the $1 million-plus stock division with +334.8%, then a contest record. (PR Newswire, 2022 · Business Wire, 2022)

## Special section

**Run the Trend Template** Type in a stock's numbers from any charting site, or keep our made-up example. The checker runs his eight tests and shows which pass.

**The VCP in four steps**

- A Stage 2 stock: it must already pass the Trend Template.
- It pauses and pulls back, then each pullback is smaller than the last.
- Volume dries up as the swings tighten.
- Buy the breakout above the last tight range, with a stop just below it.

**Leader or laggard? Tap your answer**

- A share is down 60% from its high and looks cheap. Answer: Laggard. He avoids stocks far from their highs; the template wants within 25% of the 52-week high.
- A share is 5% below its yearly high, above its rising 50-, 150- and 200-day averages. Answer: Leader. That is the Trend Template's picture of a Stage 2 leader.
- A share's 200-day average has been falling for six months, but it jumped 10% today. Answer: Laggard. He wants the 200-day average rising for at least a month; one good day does not change the trend.
- A rising share pulls back 20%, then 10%, then 4%, on lighter and lighter volume. Answer: Leader. Shrinking pullbacks on drying volume are his VCP.

## Words to know

- **Moving average**: The average closing price over a set number of days, redrawn each day.
- **52-week high/low**: The highest and lowest prices of the past year.
- **Relative strength**: How a stock's price has done compared with other stocks, often as a 1 to 99 rank.
- **Stage 2**: In Stan Weinstein's scheme, the rising phase of a stock's cycle.
- **VCP**: Volatility Contraction Pattern: a pause in which each pullback is smaller than the last.
- **Pivot**: The price level whose breakout is the buy signal.
- **Stop loss**: A preset price at which you sell to limit a loss.
- **SEPA**: Specific Entry Point Analysis, his name for his whole method.
