# Allan Gray

> Rose 10% at Fidelity while the 1962 market crashed, founded South Africa's biggest private fund firm and Orbis, then gave his control to charity.

Source page: https://alienspacex.com/gray.html · Section: the wider world · Updated 2026-11-28

## Core idea

Be contrarian. Look where others will not: cheap, neglected, unloved.

## Summary

Allan Gray grew up in South Africa, studied at Harvard, and made his name at Fidelity in Boston in 1962, when the market fell 21.7% in a month and his portfolio rose. In 1973 he went home to Cape Town and started a firm that became South Africa's largest private investment manager, then founded Orbis to invest worldwide. He shunned publicity, asked more questions than he answered, and gave his controlling stake in both firms to a charitable foundation.

## Record at a glance

- **+10%**: his Fidelity portfolio in 1962, while the market fell 21.7% in January
- **1973**: the year he started his own firm in Cape Town
- **19.2%**: a year: the firm's flagship equity fund since 1998, against 14.4% for its benchmark
- **100%**: of the family's controlling stake given to a charitable foundation in 2015

## Big ideas

### 1. Butterworth to Harvard

He came from a pioneering family in the Eastern Cape, overcame dyslexia, and studied at Rhodes, the LSE and Harvard.

- **Pioneers.** His family came from Aberdeen in the 1890s; his grandmother became South Africa's first woman mayor. (Daily Maverick)
- **Dyslexic.** He was dyslexic, and still went on to study accounting at Rhodes University. (Daily Maverick)
- **Abroad.** He studied at the London School of Economics and took an MBA at Harvard. (Daily Maverick · Wikipedia)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/gray-01-butterworth-to-harvard.html

### 2. 1962

At Fidelity in Boston, the market fell 21.7% in January 1962 and stayed weak until October. His portfolio rose 10%.

- **Eight years.** He spent eight years at Fidelity in Boston. (Daily Maverick · Wikipedia)
- **The crash.** In 1962 the market fell 21.7% in January; his portfolio ended up 10%. (Daily Maverick)
- **A foundation.** The experience made contrarian thinking the base of his philosophy for life. (Daily Maverick)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/gray-02-1962.html

### 3. Chosen, not sold

In 1973 he started his own firm in Cape Town, believing clients should choose its services rather than be sold them.

- **1973.** He founded Allan Gray Investment Counsel in Cape Town. (Letter, 2015 · Wikipedia)
- **Not sold.** He wanted services clients chose, so the firm's growth would prove it was helping them. (Letter, 2015)
- **The ghost.** So private was he that he was called the 'ghost of Cape Town'. (Daily Maverick)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/gray-03-chosen-not-sold.html

### 4. Contrarian by nature

He looked for cheap, neglected companies others overlooked, and trusted his own judgment over the crowd's mood.

- **Neglected.** He hunted for cheap companies the market had forgotten. (Legacy)
- **Argue the bull case.** When a share was beaten down he pushed colleagues to make the case for it. (Legacy)
- **Not right more often.** Being contrarian does not make you right more often; it finds prices where bad news is already in. (Orbis)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/gray-04-contrarian-by-nature.html

### 5. Cycles

He believed in cycles: good times breed bad times, and strong businesses bought near a cyclical low offer a discount and improving results.

- **Good times, bad times.** He often said that good times, by their nature, produce the tough times. (Legacy)
- **Buy near the low.** Strong businesses near a cyclical low give both a discount and better results ahead. (Legacy)
- **And compounders.** He also liked businesses that could compound for a very long time. (Legacy)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/gray-05-cycles.html

### 6. Value, then dream a little

He stood firmly on what a business was worth, then looked for the upside the market was not pricing.

- **A firm base.** Investments rested on business value. (Legacy)
- **Blue sky.** He looked for undervalued potential the market had not priced. (Legacy)
- **Dream a little.** He encouraged younger colleagues to imagine what could go right. (Legacy)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/gray-06-value-then-dream-a-little.html

### 7. Wrong, and changing your mind

He accepted being wrong sometimes, because good calls would more than pay for bad ones, and he changed his mind when the facts changed.

- **Asymmetry.** He accepted some mistakes because the right decisions could more than offset smaller losses. (Legacy)
- **Held lightly.** He sought out contrary evidence and reversed positions when the facts changed. (Legacy)
- **Ideas go stale.** He taught that a good investment idea loses its value quickly if not acted on. (Legacy)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/gray-07-wrong-and-changing-your-mind.html

### 8. The price of being different

He warned that a long-term contrarian approach pays only for those who can bear long, uncomfortable spells of underperformance.

- **The catch.** Superior results come only if you can withstand uncomfortably long periods of lagging. (Letter, 2015)
- **Shared conviction.** Owners and decision-makers must share the conviction to stay the course. (Letter, 2015)
- **The record.** The firm's flagship equity fund has returned about 19.2% a year since 1998, against 14.4% for its benchmark. (Factsheet, 2026)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/gray-08-the-price-of-being-different.html

### 9. Fees that serve clients

His firms tied their fees to results, so they earned more only when clients did, an idea Orbis took further with refundable fees.

- **Performance fees.** The flagship fund's fee rises and falls with its result against the benchmark. (Factsheet, 2026)
- **Refundable.** Orbis is known for client-aligned, refundable fees. (Orbis, 2021)
- **Client wealth.** Colleagues recall that he insisted good returns must turn into real wealth for clients. (Legacy)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/gray-09-fees-that-serve-clients.html

### 10. Giving it all back

He funded students' education for years, then gave his family's controlling stakes in both firms to a charitable foundation.

- **Scholarships.** In 2005 he created a foundation for school and university scholarships across southern Africa. (Daily Maverick)
- **Control given away.** In 2015 the family gave its controlling stakes in Allan Gray and Orbis to a foundation. (Letter, 2015 · BusinessLive)
- **Why.** He said free enterprise had done much for many, and those it rewards should help others. (Daily Maverick)
- **Long-term owners.** He wanted the firms free from short-term pressure from outside owners. (Letter, 2015)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/gray-10-giving-it-all-back.html

## Special section

**Crowd or contrarian?** 

**Five lessons he left**

- Look where others won't: neglected, cheap, unloved.
- Think in cycles: good times make bad times.
- Stand on value, then dream a little.
- Expect to be wrong sometimes, and size for it.
- Bear the lean years: they are the price of being different.

## Words to know

- **Contrarian**: Going against the crowd: buying what others avoid, selling what they chase.
- **Cycle**: The repeating rise and fall of an industry or market.
- **Compounder**: A business that keeps growing its value at a high rate for many years.
- **Benchmark**: The index or average a fund is compared with.
- **Performance fee**: A fee that rises when a fund beats its benchmark and falls when it lags.
- **Refundable fee**: A performance fee held back and returned if later results fall short.
- **Underperformance**: Doing worse than the benchmark.
