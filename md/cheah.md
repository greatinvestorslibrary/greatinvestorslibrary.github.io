# Cheah Cheng Hye

> Sold pineapples as a boy in Penang, spent 17 years as a journalist, then built Value Partners from under US$5 million: Asia's Warren Buffett.

Source page: https://alienspacex.com/cheah.html · Section: the wider world · Updated 2026-11-28

## Core idea

Do the legwork. Visit companies; spend little time in the office.

## Summary

Cheah Cheng Hye grew up poor in Penang, sold pineapples by the road after his father died, and never went to university. He became a financial journalist in Hong Kong, taught himself accounting, and in 1993 co-founded Value Partners with less than US$5 million. Its Classic Fund rose about 3,588% over the next 32 years, against 722% for the Hang Seng Index. Asia calls him its Warren Buffett.

## Record at a glance

- **< US$5m**: Value Partners' money under management when it opened in 1993
- **+3,588%**: Classic Fund, April 1993 to January 2025, against +722% for the Hang Seng Index
- **2,500**: company visits a year by his firm's analysts
- **7 of 25**: years in which the Classic Fund lost money, to 2018

## Big ideas

### 1. Pineapples in Penang

He grew up poor in Penang. When his father died he was nine, the eldest, and the family's breadwinner.

- **Poor and hungry.** He was born in Penang in 1954 to a poor family and often went hungry. (The Edge)
- **Breadwinner at nine.** His father died when he was nine; as the eldest he supported the family. (The Edge)
- **Street trades.** He sold pineapples by the road, folded newspapers, and sold joss sticks at a temple. (The Edge · Time, 2007)

### 2. Seventeen years of journalism

At 17 he joined a Malaysian newspaper, moved to Hong Kong in 1974, and spent 17 years reporting on Asian business.

- **A folder first.** He joined The Star at 17 folding newspapers, then became a reporter. (The Star, 2019)
- **Hong Kong.** He moved to Hong Kong in 1974 and worked for the Asian Wall Street Journal and Asiaweek. (The Edge · The Star, 2019)
- **A scoop.** He was first to report that the Hong Kong dollar would be pegged to the US dollar in 1983. (China Daily)
- **Why it helps.** He has said being an ex-journalist helps in investing. (Inst. Investor)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/cheah-02-seventeen-years-of-journalism.html

### 3. Self-taught

With no university degree, he wrote himself a study plan in accounting, law and statistics, and read annual reports cover to cover.

- **No college.** He never went to university. (The Edge)
- **A study plan.** He set himself a course in accountancy, law and statistics. (The Edge)
- **Annual reports.** He read companies' reports from cover to cover and worked long hours. (The Edge)
- **A trader first.** In 1989 he became head of research and a trader at Morgan Grenfell in Hong Kong. (MoneyWeek)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/cheah-03-self-taught.html

### 4. A hobby fund becomes a giant

In 1993 he and a partner started Value Partners with under US$5 million. In 2007 it became Hong Kong's first listed asset manager.

- **1993.** He co-founded Value Partners with V-Nee Yeh, with less than US$5 million. (The Star, 2019 · MoneyWeek)
- **A listing.** Its 2007 share offer drew demand for more than 300 times the shares available. (SCMP, 2007)
- **The record.** The Classic Fund rose about 3,588% from April 1993 to January 2025, against 722% for the Hang Seng. (Wikipedia)

### 5. Graham, adapted for Asia

He calls himself an old-fashioned Graham-and-Dodd value investor, but puts more weight on cash flow and governance, which matter more in emerging markets.

- **Old-fashioned.** He describes himself as a value investor in the Graham-and-Dodd tradition. (MoneyWeek)
- **Cash flow.** He weighs cash flow more heavily than Graham did. (MoneyWeek)
- **Governance.** In emerging markets he gives corporate governance special weight. (MoneyWeek)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/cheah-05-graham-adapted-for-asia.html

### 6. Two thousand five hundred visits

His firm's analysts make about 2,500 company visits a year and are told to spend little time in the office.

- **Legwork.** Value Partners makes about 2,500 company visits a year. (The Edge · MoneyWeek)
- **Out of the office.** Staff are encouraged to spend little time at their desks. (The Edge)
- **BYD.** After BYD's shares fell sharply he visited, judged it unfairly marked down, and became its second-largest shareholder. (MoneyWeek)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/cheah-06-two-thousand-five-hundred-visits.html

### 7. Buy when others sell

He buys when people sell and sells when they buy, prefers dull shares, and stayed out of the dot-com mania.

- **The rule.** We buy when people sell and sell when people buy, he says. (The Edge)
- **Dull is good.** He prefers dull stocks when others chase exciting ones. (The Edge)
- **No dot-coms.** He bought no dot-com shares while the market chased them. (MoneyWeek)
- **Sold to Buffett's fans.** When Buffett invested in BYD in 2008 and the market followed, Value Partners sold for a large profit. (MoneyWeek)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/cheah-07-buy-when-others-sell.html

### 8. Stupid-clever

He says ego is an investor's worst enemy, and that about a third of his decisions are mistakes, mostly about people.

- **Kill the ego.** He urges a 'stupid-clever' mindset that takes the self out of investment decisions. (MoneyWeek)
- **A third wrong.** He reckons about a third of his decisions are mistakes, mainly from misjudging managers. (The Edge)
- **Too trusting.** He admits his trusting nature can be a weakness in a fund manager. (The Edge)
- **An airline.** He put US$30 million into a Hong Kong airline that went bust within six months. (MoneyWeek)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/cheah-08-stupid-clever.html

### 9. Losing years, and the regulator

His fund lost money in seven of its first 25 years. His record also includes a 2000 public reprimand and a 2017 fine for the firm.

- **Seven bad years.** Value investing is not always in favour: the Classic Fund was in the red in seven of 25 years. (China Daily)
- **The 2000 reprimand.** Hong Kong's regulator reprimanded him and the firm over buy orders near the close in late December 1998. (SFC, 2000)
- **Unintentional, but.** It found the effect unintentional, but said he should have known it could affect prices. (SFC, 2000)
- **The 2017 fine.** The firm was fined HK$4 million when two funds issued more shares than allowed; no investor losses were apparent. (SFC, 2017)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/cheah-09-losing-years-and-the-regulator.html

### 10. The lucky unlucky boy

He credits luck and an era of fast growth as much as skill, and stepped back from the firm in 2025.

- **Luck.** He says the unlucky boy was very lucky, but did not know it then. (The Edge)
- **Hard work.** He believes working harder can make up for a rival's cleverness. (The Edge)
- **Stepping back.** In January 2025, as a Chinese broker took control of the firm, he stepped down as co-chairman. (Hedgeweek, 2025 · Wikipedia)

## Special section

**Would he buy, or wait? Tap your answer**

- A sound company's shares have halved on bad news you think is overdone. You have visited and checked. Answer: Buy. Buying a sound business after an unfair markdown, having done the legwork, is the BYD pattern.
- Everyone wants a hot new sector, and prices have tripled in a year. Answer: Wait. He stayed out of the dot-com mania: the crowd's favourite is rarely cheap.
- A dull, cash-rich company trades cheaply, and nobody covers it. Answer: Buy. He prefers dull stocks when others chase exciting ones.
- The numbers look cheap, but you have doubts about whether the managers can be trusted. Answer: Wait. He says most of his mistakes came from misjudging management.

**An honest note.** On 5 October 2000 Hong Kong's Securities and Futures Commission publicly reprimanded Value Partners Limited and Cheah Cheng Hye, the person responsible for its investment decisions. It found that on 28, 29 and 31 December 1998 the firm had placed buy orders near the close of trading in five stocks, which left their closing prices higher than they might otherwise have been. The SFC found this unintentional but said he should have known it could affect prices, and cited inadequate controls. In January 2017 the SFC reprimanded two Value Partners companies and fined them HK$4 million in total because two funds had issued more shares than their constitutions allowed, and the firm reported this six months late; the SFC found no apparent investor losses, and he was not named. We include this because the library puts integrity first and tells readers what is on the public record.

## Words to know

- **Value investing**: Buying shares for less than the business is worth.
- **Graham and Dodd**: Authors of Security Analysis (1934), the founding text of value investing.
- **Cash flow**: Cash a business actually brings in, as opposed to accounting profit.
- **Corporate governance**: How a company is controlled and whether minority shareholders are treated fairly.
- **Hang Seng Index**: Hong Kong's main share index.
- **Public reprimand**: A formal, published rebuke from a regulator.
- **Closing price**: The last traded price of the day, used to value funds.
