# Francisco García Paramés

> Taught himself value investing from Peter Lynch and Buffett's letters, then beat Spain's market for 21 years, survived his worst years, and kept going.

Source page: https://alienspacex.com/parames.html · Section: the wider world · Updated 2026-11-28

## Core idea

Self-taught. Read the masters, then think for yourself.

## Summary

Francisco García Paramés taught himself value investing from Peter Lynch's book and a letter he requested from Warren Buffett. From 1993 to 2014 his Spanish fund at Bestinver returned about 15.7% a year, more than five times its benchmark in total. He left over a disagreement with the owners, started Cobas in 2017, lived through years of painful losses, and stuck to his method. Spain calls him its Warren Buffett.

## Record at a glance

- **15.7%**: a year: his Spanish fund at Bestinver, 1993 to 2014
- **2,279%**: the fund's total gain over that time, against 410% for its benchmark
- **−14.1%**: his first year running a global fund, 1998, while markets rose 16.5%
- **20%**: a year: Cobas's international fund over the five years to mid-2026

## Big ideas

### 1. An uninspired start

He was an average student and a reluctant shop trainee. A magazine review of Peter Lynch's book changed his life.

- **Galicia.** He was born in 1963 in El Ferrol, in north-western Spain, one of five children. (MOI Global)
- **A first job.** He spent 15 months as a graduate trainee at the retailer El Corte Inglés, then took an MBA. (MOI Global)
- **The spark.** After reading a review in Business Week he picked up One Up on Wall Street, and then the other masters. (MOI Global)
- **A letter from Omaha.** He wrote to Buffett's company to get a copy of the shareholder letter. (MOI Global)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/parames-01-an-uninspired-start.html

### 2. Self-taught at Bestinver

He joined a small fund firm owned by an industrial group as a young analyst, and within two years was running its Spanish fund.

- **A quiet start.** He joined Bestinver, part of the Acciona group, as a young analyst. (Interview, 2017 · MOI Global)
- **Learning by doing.** When his first tasks slowed down he began analysing Spanish shares to keep busy. (CFA, 2013)
- **Fully invested.** His approach meant being fully in shares, not timing the market. (Interview, 2017)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/parames-02-self-taught-at-bestinver.html

### 3. Twenty-one years of results

From 1993 to 2014 his Spanish fund returned about 15.7% a year: 2,279% in total, against 410% for its benchmark.

- **The record.** Bestinfond gained 2,279% from 1993 to 2014, about 15.7% a year, against 410% for its index. (Interview, 2017)
- **Thirty times.** That turned each euro into about thirty. (MOI Global)
- **Abroad too.** His global fund beat the world index even when the index went nowhere. (Interview, 2017)
- **A loyal following.** When he left Bestinver in 2014, its assets fell by about 30%. (MoneyWeek)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/parames-03-twenty-one-years-of-results.html

### 4. Austrian economics

Ten years into his career he discovered the Austrian school of economics. It became his compass: markets work, are never in balance, and growth comes from saving.

- **A turning point.** Finding the Austrian economists ten years in was, he says, a major turning point. (Book, 2018)
- **Markets work.** He starts from the idea that markets work, by definition, and are never in equilibrium. (Mises, 2018)
- **Saving drives growth.** Economic growth comes from rising productivity, financed by savings. (Mises, 2018)
- **No guessing.** He sees trying to guess the economy's next move as close to a stab in the dark. (Book, 2018)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/parames-04-austrian-economics.html

### 5. Risk is losing money

He defines risk not as how much a price swings, but as the chance of losing money.

- **Not volatility.** Risk is the probability of losing money by holding an asset, not how much its price moves. (Mises, 2018)
- **Time helps.** His book sets out long-run data on shares against other assets to argue for a long view. (Mises, 2018 · Book, 2018)
- **Painful patches.** Even his best decades held hard stretches, such as a 22% fall in six months in 2011. (Letter, 2018)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/parames-05-risk-is-losing-money.html

### 6. Quality at a low price

He looks for strong businesses with high returns on capital, growth and little debt, bought when they are cheap.

- **Return on capital.** Bestinver judged business quality by return on capital employed. (CFA, 2013)
- **Governance.** The managers paid special attention to how companies were governed. (CFA, 2013)
- **The 2019 portfolio.** Cobas described its holdings as businesses with strong competitive positions, average returns on capital of about 25%, growth, and little debt. (Letter, 2019)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/parames-06-quality-at-a-low-price.html

### 7. Leaving, and starting again

In 2014 he left Bestinver over differences with its owners. After a two-year wait he launched Cobas in 2017.

- **A clean break.** He left Bestinver in September 2014 after disagreements with its owners. (MoneyWeek)
- **Two years out.** A non-compete agreement kept him out of fund management for two years. (MoneyWeek)
- **A first bet.** He later said he saw an extraordinary opportunity in a beaten-down miner in late 2015. (MoneyWeek)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/parames-07-leaving-and-starting-again.html

### 8. The hard years

Cobas had a brutal start: losses in 2018, then the worst period for value investing on record, and the Covid crash. He wrote honestly to his investors.

- **2018.** His funds fell sharply, hit by holdings such as shipping companies. (Letter, 2018)
- **It had happened before.** He reminded investors that his first global year, 1998, lost 14.1% while markets rose 16.5%. (Letter, 2018)
- **2020.** In the Covid crash he acknowledged that most of his investors were suffering big losses in hard-earned savings. (Letter, 2020)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/parames-08-the-hard-years.html

### 9. The rebound

By mid-2026 his international fund had returned about 20% a year over five years, and Madrid had become Europe's value-investing capital.

- **Five good years.** Bloomberg reported his international fund up about 20% a year over the five years to July 2026. (Bloomberg, 2026)
- **His method.** He describes it simply: buy assets below their intrinsic value, and be patient. (Bloomberg, 2026)
- **Disciples.** Former colleagues and students run their own value funds, making Madrid a hub. (Bloomberg, 2026)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/parames-09-the-rebound.html

### 10. We are qualified to wait

His last lesson is patience: the best returns come to those who can wait, and to managers whose clients let them.

- **Waiting.** He calls waiting the most important thing, and says his team is qualified to do it. (Mises, 2018)
- **No forecasts.** He does not try to predict markets or economies, only to understand companies. (Interview, 2017 · Bloomberg, 2026)
- **Patient clients.** In the 2020 crash he thanked unitholders who kept their trust, some of whom invested more. (Letter, 2020)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/parames-10-we-are-qualified-to-wait.html

## Special section

**Would it pass his checklist?**

- It earns a high return on the capital it uses
- It has a strong competitive position that rivals find hard to copy
- It is still growing, not shrinking
- It carries little debt, or none
- It is run well, with good governance
- Its price is well below what you think it is worth
- You can hold it for years without needing the money

## Words to know

- **Value investing**: Buying shares for less than the business is worth, then waiting.
- **Austrian school**: A school of economics, from Mises and Hayek, that stresses markets, saving and the limits of forecasting.
- **ROCE**: Return on capital employed: profit as a share of the money tied up in a business.
- **Benchmark**: The index a fund is compared with.
- **Non-compete**: A contract term that bars someone from working for a rival for a period.
- **Intrinsic value**: What a business is really worth, based on the cash it will produce.
- **Volatility**: How much a price swings up and down.
