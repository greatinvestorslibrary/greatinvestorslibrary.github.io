# Seiroku Honda

> A poor farmer's son who saved a quarter of every pay cheque from 25, invested it, and gave almost all of it away when he retired.

Source page: https://alienspacex.com/honda.html · Section: Japan · Updated 2026-11-28

## Core idea

Save first. Set aside a quarter the day the money arrives, before spending anything.

## Summary

Seiroku Honda was born poor in 1866, became Japan's first doctor of forestry, and designed the forest of Meiji Shrine and parks across Japan. From the age of 25 he saved a quarter of every pay cheque before spending a yen, invested the savings in shares and forest land, and by 40 earned more from interest and dividends than from his salary. When he retired he gave almost all of it away. His 1950 book, My Confession of Wealth, is still read by Japanese savers today.

## Record at a glance

- **¼**: of every pay cheque saved first, from age 25, plus every bonus and windfall
- **Age 40**: when interest and dividends passed his professor's salary
- **30+**: industries he owned shares in: railways, gas, power, paper, beer, spinning, cement and more
- **2,600 ha**: of Chichibu forest he gave to Saitama, whose income still funds student loans

## Big ideas

### 1. A poor farmer's son

He was born poor in 1866, studied his way up, and became Japan's first doctor of forestry and the designer of famous parks and forests.

- **Humble start.** He was born in 1866 into a poor farming family in what is now Kuki, Saitama, and studied through hardship. (JPX, education · Saitama, 2024)
- **First of his kind.** He became Japan's first doctor of forestry and a professor at the Imperial University in Tokyo. (Rakuten, 2018 · Saitama, 2024)
- **Father of parks.** He designed parks across Japan, including Hibiya Park in Tokyo, and the forest of Meiji Shrine. (MUFG Bank, 2020 · Saitama, 2024)
- **Remembered for money.** Today he is remembered at least as much for how he saved and invested as for his parks. (MUFG Bank, 2020)

### 2. The quarter rule

Every time pay arrived, he set aside a quarter before spending anything, and saved every bonus and windfall in full.

- **First, not last.** He saved a quarter of each pay cheque the day it arrived, treating it as money he had never received. (JPX, education · MUFG Bank, 2020)
- **Windfalls too.** Bonuses and other one-off income went entirely into savings. (JPX, education · President)
- **The harvest image.** In his words, if you earn 40 yen, think you received 30 and put 10 away. (JPX, education)
- **Doing, not knowing.** He insisted that saving is a matter of doing it, not of finding the right method. (Daiwa, 2021)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/honda-02-the-quarter-rule.html

### 3. Hard years by choice

The rule hurt. With a large household, money ran out each month. He held firm, choosing hardship early rather than being forced into it later.

- **Salt and sesame.** At the end of some months the family ate rice with only salt and sesame, and the children cried. (JPX, education)
- **No exceptions.** He persuaded his family that the rule was right and would not let feeling override it. (JPX, education · Daiwa, 2021)
- **Thrift by choice.** He argued that you should not cut back because poverty forces you to, but save on purpose to overpower poverty. (President · JPX, education)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/honda-03-hard-years-by-choice.html

### 4. Vanity is the enemy

He saw the wish to look well-off as the biggest obstacle to saving, and believed early hardship makes for a better life later.

- **Vanity.** Wanting to live better than others, or just to look as if you do, is what wrecks saving. (JPX, education)
- **Spoiled young, poor later.** He wrote that those raised in luxury tend to become poor, while those who meet poverty early do better later. (JPX, education)
- **Pass it early.** Since everyone goes through a poor stretch at some point, he thought it best to get it over with young. (JPX, education)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/honda-04-vanity-is-the-enemy.html

### 5. The snowball

Savings, once they form a core, grow by themselves. By 40 his interest and dividends earned more than his salary.

- **Like a snowball.** He wrote that money is like a snowball: tiny at first, but once a core forms it grows remarkably. (President)
- **A small start.** His first monthly saving was 14 yen and 50 sen. (President)
- **Pay and interest together.** After two or three years, interest began to arrive, so life slowly became easier. (JPX, education)
- **The milestone.** Fifteen years in, at 40, his interest and dividends were far larger than his salary. (President · Rakuten, 2018)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/honda-05-the-snowball.html

### 6. Own good businesses, widely

He put his savings into the sound companies of his day, starting with railways and spreading across more than thirty industries.

- **Thirty railway shares.** His first purchase was 30 shares of Nippon Railway at 12.50 yen each, on a professor's advice that railways had a future. (JPX, education)
- **Bought out.** When the railways were nationalised, his shares were bought at about 2.5 times what he paid. (JPX, education)
- **Spread out.** As railway prospects dimmed he moved on, owning good shares across more than 30 industries, from gas and power to beer and cement. (President · JPX, education)
- **No gambling.** He kept away from speculation and stuck to sound investment. (JPX, education)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/honda-06-own-good-businesses-widely.html

### 7. Invest in what you know: his forest

As a forester, he understood land and trees. He bought natural forest in the Chichibu mountains, and it became part of his fortune, and later his gift.

- **His own field.** He bought natural forest in the Chichibu mountains, close to his expertise. (JPX, education · Saitama Pref.)
- **A timber boom.** After the Russo-Japanese War timber prices soared, and he sold part of his forest at about 70 times its cost. (JPX, education)
- **2,600 hectares.** His forest holdings near Nakatsugawa in Chichibu covered about 2,600 hectares. (Saitama Pref.)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/honda-07-invest-in-what-you-know-his-forest.html

### 8. Take a modest profit, protect the principal

He followed a simple selling rule: take a 20% gain when it comes, and if a holding doubles, sell half so the original money is safe.

- **Twenty per cent.** On some positions, if he reached a 20% gain, he took it in full and put the money on deposit, without chasing more. (JPX, education)
- **Sell half at double.** If a holding more than doubled, he sold half at once, getting his original money back. (JPX, education)
- **Then relax.** With the principal safe, a later crash could not hurt him, and further gains were a bonus. (JPX, education)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/honda-08-take-a-modest-profit-protect-the-principal.html

### 9. Patience, and no shame about money

He waited patiently for good chances, invested boldly when times were bad, and rejected the old samurai pose of looking down on money.

- **Three steps.** Save a quarter; invest once a little has built up; wait patiently for good chances without forcing anything. (MUFG Bank, 2020)
- **Bad times, good chances.** Downturns pushed share prices down, and he invested actively when they did. (MUFG Bank, 2020)
- **Money matters.** He criticised the feudal habit of pretending to scorn money, calling money useful and worth having. (JPX, education)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/honda-09-patience-and-no-shame-about-money.html

### 10. Give it back

At 60 he retired and gave almost everything away, mostly anonymously. His forest still pays for students' education in Saitama.

- **Wealth is not happiness.** He concluded that great wealth and fame do not guarantee happiness. (JPX, education)
- **Almost everything.** On retiring in 1927 he gave nearly all his assets to public causes, mostly without his name. (JPX, education · Saitama Pref.)
- **A forest for students.** Saitama's scholarship in his name, funded by income from his donated forest, has lent to students since 1954. (Saitama Pref.)
- **Still lending.** In 2024 it still offered interest-free loans of up to 30,000 yen a month to Saitama students. (Saitama, 2024)

## Words to know

- **Tenbiki (天引き)**: Deducting money at source, before you can spend it.
- **Meiji era**: 1868 to 1912: Japan's rapid modernisation under Emperor Meiji.
- **Sen (銭)**: A hundredth of a yen, used in his day.
- **Nippon Railway**: A private railway company of the Meiji era, nationalised in 1906.
- **Hectare**: 10,000 square metres, about 2.5 acres.
- **Bushi kuwanedo takayōji**: 'A samurai picks his teeth even when he hasn't eaten': keeping up appearances while pretending to scorn money.
- **Dividend**: The share of profit a company pays to its shareholders.
