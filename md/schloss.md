# Walter Schloss

> Never went to college, never visited a company, owned a hundred cheap shares at a time, and beat the market for 47 years.

Source page: https://alienspacex.com/schloss.html · Section: the United States · Updated 2026-11-28

## Core idea

Price first. Price, compared with value, is what matters most.

## Summary

Walter Schloss never went to college. He started as a Wall Street runner at 18, learned from Benjamin Graham at evening classes and worked for him before starting his own partnership in 1955. For 47 years he bought cheap shares by simple rules, owned around a hundred at a time, never visited managements and beat the market by a wide margin. Buffett called him one of the good guys of Wall Street.

## Record at a glance

- **21.3% a year**: his partnership, 1956 to 1984, before his share (16.1% for his partners), as Buffett reported
- **47 years**: 1956 to 2002, about 15% to 16% a year, by different counts
- **$100,000**: the money 19 investors gave him to start in 1955
- **≈ $11,000**: his office costs in a year when his partnership earned $19 million, as Buffett noted

## Big ideas

### 1. Price comes first

His first factor: price, compared with value, is the most important thing. A share is part of a business, not a piece of paper.

- **Factor one.** Price, set against value, is the most important factor of all. (Note, 1994)
- **A real business.** Try to work out what the company is worth, remembering that a share is a piece of a business. (Note, 1994)
- **Cheap against value.** Buffett said Schloss simply buys businesses selling for less than they are worth, and that's all he does. (Buffett, 1984)
- **Graham's methods.** He picked shares by simple statistical methods learned while working for Graham. (Buffett, 2007)

### 2. Assets over earnings

He preferred buying assets at a discount to buying earnings. Earnings can change fast; assets usually change slowly.

- **Start with book value.** Use book value as the starting point for estimating what a business is worth. (Note, 1994)
- **Watch the debt.** Make sure debt is well below the shareholders' equity. (Note, 1994)
- **Assets change slowly.** Earnings can change dramatically in a short time; you need to know far more about a company to buy its earnings. (Note, 1994)
- **Not growth or managers.** Unlike most analysts, he paid little attention to earnings growth or to management. (Morningstar)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/schloss-02-assets-over-earnings.html

### 3. Buy near the lows

He liked shares trading near the bottom of their range of recent years, and he was patient once he had bought.

- **Near the low.** He found it helpful to buy near the low of the past few years. (Note, 1994)
- **Check the history.** A share that has fallen from 125 to 60 may look cheap, but if it was 20 three years ago, it may still be vulnerable. (Note, 1994)
- **Patience.** Stocks don't go up immediately; give them time. (Note, 1994)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/schloss-03-buy-near-the-lows.html

### 4. Own many, buy in steps

He spread his money across around a hundred shares and bought and sold gradually rather than all at once.

- **Over a hundred.** Buffett noted that Schloss owned well over 100 stocks at a time. (Buffett, 1984)
- **On a scale.** Buy on a scale and sell on a scale: in steps, not all at once. (Note, 1994)
- **No single bet.** A few big winners did not explain his success; it came from many modest ones. (Buffett, 2007)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/schloss-04-own-many-buy-in-steps.html

### 5. Public numbers only

He never visited managements or chased inside information. He read the published numbers, sent for annual reports and decided for himself.

- **Never near inside information.** Buffett wrote that Walter and Edwin never came within a mile of inside information and used outside information only sparingly. (Buffett, 2007)
- **The manuals.** He looked up the numbers in the manuals and sent for the annual reports. (Buffett, 1984)
- **A tiny office.** He worked without a secretary, clerk or bookkeeper; his only associate was his son. (Buffett, 2007)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/schloss-05-public-numbers-only.html

### 6. Keep emotions out

Fear and greed are the worst emotions for an investor. Have your own method, follow it calmly, and don't be swayed by tips or crowds.

- **Fear and greed.** Try not to let emotions affect your judgment; fear and greed are the worst of them. (Note, 1994)
- **No tips.** Don't buy on tips or for a quick move, and don't sell on bad news. (Note, 1994)
- **Be a loner.** Don't be afraid to stand alone, but look hard for weaknesses in your own thinking. (Note, 1994)
- **Have a philosophy.** Have a method of investing and stick to it once you have decided. (Note, 1994)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/schloss-06-keep-emotions-out.html

### 7. Don't sell too soon

Many investors sell after a quick gain. He re-checked the company's value first, and watched the level of the whole market.

- **Not in a hurry.** A 50% gain is not by itself a reason to sell; re-check the company's value against its book value first. (Note, 1994)
- **The market's level.** Before selling, ask whether yields are low, prices high and people very optimistic. (Note, 1994)
- **Advice is optional.** Listen to people you respect, but remember it's your money and the decision is yours. (Note, 1994)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/schloss-07-dont-sell-too-soon.html

### 8. Compounding, shares and leverage

Let returns compound, prefer shares to bonds over the long run, and be careful with borrowed money.

- **The rule of 72.** At 12% a year, money doubles in about six years: divide 72 by your return. (Note, 1994)
- **Shares over bonds.** Bonds limit your gains and inflation eats their value, so he preferred shares. (Note, 1994)
- **Leverage.** Be careful with borrowed money; it can go against you. (Note, 1994)
- **Keep what you make.** It is harder to keep money than to make it, and a big loss is hard to recover. (Note, 1994)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/schloss-08-compounding-shares-and-leverage.html

### 9. Paid only when partners gained

He charged no management fee. He took a share of profits only, and kept his costs tiny.

- **No fee.** He charged no management fee, taking a 25% share of the profits instead. (Morningstar)
- **Not a dime.** Buffett wrote that he took nothing unless his partners made money. (Buffett, 2007)
- **$11,000.** Buffett once pointed out that his office costs were about $11,000 in a year when the partnership earned $19 million. (Morningstar)
- **Skin in the game.** He invested his own money alongside his partners. (Morningstar · Buffett, 2007)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/schloss-09-paid-only-when-partners-gained.html

### 10. A quiet life on Wall Street

He came in as a teenage runner, learned from Graham, served in the war and ran his partnership for 47 years with his son. Buffett used his record to show that skill, not luck, can beat the market.

- **No college.** He started as a runner at 18 in 1934; his firm paid for two courses with Graham at the New York Stock Exchange Institute. (Obituary, 2012)
- **War and Graham-Newman.** He served four years in the Army, kept in touch with Graham, and joined Graham-Newman in 1946. (Obituary, 2012)
- **His own firm.** He left in 1955 and started with $100,000 from 19 investors; his son Edwin joined in 1973. (Obituary, 2012 · Interview, 1989)
- **Not luck.** Buffett wrote that there was no possibility his 47-year record was due to chance, and that it involved no real risk of permanent loss. (Buffett, 2007)

## Special section

**His 16 factors, in our words** 

**Would it pass a look in his style?**

- The price is at or below book value
- The price is near the low of the past few years
- Debt is well below shareholders' equity
- The company has a long record, good and bad years included
- You can judge it from published reports alone
- It would be one of many holdings, not a big bet

## Words to know

- **Book value**: A company's assets minus its liabilities, as shown in its accounts.
- **Runner**: A junior clerk who carried papers and messages between Wall Street firms.
- **Partnership**: A pooled investment fund run by a manager for a group of partners.
- **Leverage**: Investing with borrowed money.
- **Rule of 72**: Divide 72 by a yearly return to find roughly how many years money takes to double.
- **Inside information**: Important facts about a company not yet made public; trading on it is illegal.
