# André Kostolany

> A speculator, not a value investor: rich twice, broke in between, and the man who taught Germany how crowds move markets.

Source page: https://alienspacex.com/kostolany.html · Section: the wider world · Updated 2026-11-28

## Core idea

Money + psychology. Prices follow the supply of money and the mood of the crowd.

## Summary

André Kostolany was born in Budapest in 1906, learned the stock market in Paris, fled to America in 1940, and spent his last decades explaining markets to Germans in books, columns and on television. He was not a value investor but a speculator, and he said so. He grew rich twice, lost nearly everything in between, and claimed to be right only 51% of the time. His thirteen books sold about three million copies, yet the English-speaking world barely knows him.

## Record at a glance

- **1906–1999**: seventy-plus years in markets, from Paris in the 1920s to Munich in the 1990s
- **2×**: rich twice: betting on the 1929 crash, then on German bonds after the war
- **51%**: the share of his trades he said were right; 49% were wrong
- **3 million**: copies of his 13 books sold, plus over 400 magazine columns

## Big ideas

### 1. From Budapest to the Paris bourse

A wealthy boy who wanted to be an art critic, he was sent by his father to learn broking in Paris in the 1920s.

- **Not his first wish.** He studied philosophy and art history and hoped to become an art critic. (etf.capital · Kagels)
- **Paris.** His father sent him to Paris in the mid-1920s to train under the broker Adrien Perquel. (comdirect · etf.capital)
- **No economics.** He never studied classical economics and mocked economists' forecasts all his life. (comdirect)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/kostolany-01-from-budapest-to-the-paris-bourse.html

### 2. Rich twice, broke in between

He made a fortune betting on falling prices around 1929, lost it, and came close to bankruptcy more than once.

- **1929.** He grew rich betting that share prices would fall around the 1929 crash. (Kagels · justETF)
- **Then lost it.** Big bets that went wrong wiped him out more than once. (justETF · Kagels)
- **51 to 49.** He admitted being right on 51% of his trades and wrong on 49%. (comdirect)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/kostolany-02-rich-twice-broke-in-between.html

### 3. The German bond coup

After the war he bought old German bonds that almost no one wanted, betting that Germany would want its good name back. It did.

- **A cheap bet.** He bought pre-war German bonds in Paris for about 250 francs each. (HSBC)
- **The reason.** He reasoned that the new West Germany would repay to regain its creditworthiness. (comdirect)
- **1953.** The London Debt Agreement settled Germany's old foreign debts. (London 1953)
- **A hundredfold.** He later sold the bonds for about 35,000 francs each: his stake grew more than a hundred times. (HSBC · comdirect)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/kostolany-03-the-german-bond-coup.html

### 4. Money plus psychology

He said market trends come from two things: how much money is available, and the mood of the crowd.

- **The formula.** Trend equals money plus psychology. (FBV, guide)
- **Not more informed.** Don't assume people buying in a frenzy know more than you. (justETF)
- **Crises and rescues.** He argued that in a crisis governments and central banks act, which is why to buy then. (justETF)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/kostolany-04-money-plus-psychology.html

### 5. The egg

He drew the market cycle as an egg. Rises and falls each have three phases: correction, accompanying, exaggeration.

- **Three phases each way.** Every rise and every fall moves through correction, an accompanying phase, and exaggeration. (etf.capital · Kagels)
- **Buy in panic.** He bought in the exaggeration of a fall, when heavy selling signals panic, and added at the start of the rise. (extraETF · HSBC)
- **Sell in euphoria.** He sold in the euphoric last phase of a rise, at the latest early in the fall. (HSBC)
- **Wait in between.** In the middle phases he advised doing nothing. (extraETF)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/kostolany-05-the-egg.html

### 6. The dog and its owner

Prices relate to the economy like a dog to its owner on a walk: running ahead, falling behind, but always coming back.

- **The walk.** The share price runs ahead of the economy or lags behind, but returns to it. (etf.capital)
- **Long walks.** Over time prices follow the economy, even when short swings are wild. (etf.capital · justETF)
- **Anything can happen.** On the stock market anything is possible, even the opposite. (justETF)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/kostolany-06-the-dog-and-its-owner.html

### 7. The four Gs

He said a successful investor needs four Gs: Geld, Gedanken, Geduld and Glück. Money, thoughts, patience and luck.

- **Geld, money.** Your own money, never borrowed. (etf.capital)
- **Gedanken, thoughts.** A considered plan: goals, time span and how much risk you can take. (etf.capital · comdirect)
- **Geduld, patience.** The most important of the four. (etf.capital)
- **Glück, luck.** It cannot be forced, but without it you lose confidence, and then patience. (comdirect)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/kostolany-07-the-four-gs.html

### 8. Shaky hands and hard-boiled ones

He split investors into the shaky, who buy in euphoria and sell in panic, and the hard-boiled, who do the opposite.

- **The shaky.** They buy when prices are high and the mood is euphoric, and sell at a loss in panic. (etf.capital · comdirect)
- **The hard-boiled.** They hold when others get nervous, and buy when sentiment is at its lowest. (etf.capital)
- **Missing a G.** Anyone lacking one of the first three Gs ends up among the shaky. (etf.capital)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/kostolany-08-shaky-hands-and-hard-boiled-ones.html

### 9. Sleeping pills and trams

His most famous advice: buy good shares, take sleeping pills, and stop looking. And never chase a share, any more than a tram.

- **Sleeping pills.** Buy shares, take sleeping pills, don't look for years, and you will be rich. (extraETF · justETF)
- **Pain, then money.** Stock market profits are payment for pain: first comes the pain, then the money. (justETF)
- **Don't chase.** Never run after a tram or a share; another one always comes. (comdirect)
- **Patience pays.** He liked to quote old Frankfurt brokers: money is made by sitting still, not with the head. (FBV, guide)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/kostolany-09-sleeping-pills-and-trams.html

### 10. Teacher of a nation

In later life he explained markets to millions of Germans in columns, seminars and television, and co-founded an early asset manager.

- **Columns.** He wrote over 400 columns for the business magazine Capital. (comdirect)
- **Books.** His 13 books sold about three million copies. (comdirect)
- **Fiduka.** In 1971 he co-founded Fiduka, one of Germany's first asset managers, with Gottfried Heller. (comdirect)
- **A last warning.** Shortly before his death in 1999 he warned against the dot-com mania. (comdirect)
  Web page: https://alienspacex.com/ideas/kostolany-10-teacher-of-a-nation.html

## Special section

**Shaky hands or hard-boiled? Tap your answer**

- Prices have doubled, everyone at dinner talks about shares, and you buy for the first time. Answer: Shaky hands. Buying in euphoria, when the crowd is already in, is his picture of shaky hands.
- The market has fallen 35% on panic headlines. You buy more with spare money. Answer: Hard-boiled. Buying in the exaggeration of a fall, with your own money, is what the hard-boiled do.
- You borrowed to buy, prices drop, and the lender forces you to sell. Answer: Shaky hands. Without your own money, the first G, you cannot hold: you become shaky whether you like it or not.
- You bought a year ago, nothing has happened, and you are still waiting calmly. Answer: Hard-boiled. Patience in the quiet middle phases is the third G.
- A share you follow jumps 20% in a day; you chase it before it 'gets away'. Answer: Shaky hands. Never run after a tram or a share: another one always comes.

**The four Gs**

- Geld (money): your own, never borrowed.
- Gedanken (thoughts): a plan you have thought through.
- Geduld (patience): the most important.
- Glück (luck): it comes more often to those with the first three.

**An honest note.** Kostolany was a speculator, not a value investor, and he did not hide it. In his early years he made and lost fortunes with large, sometimes borrowed bets, and he came close to bankruptcy more than once. He published no audited record, so his wealth and many trades are known only from his own books and later accounts. We include him for his lessons on crowd psychology and patience, which have lasted, and we recommend none of his speculative methods.

## Words to know

- **Speculator**: Someone who buys or sells expecting to profit from price moves, often over shorter periods.
- **Hausse and baisse**: French and German words for a rising market and a falling one.
- **Shaky hands (zittrige Hände)**: Investors who buy in euphoria and sell in panic.
- **Hard-boiled (hartgesottene)**: Investors who act against the crowd and hold through falls.
- **Bond**: A loan to a government or company that pays interest and is repaid at the end.
- **London Debt Agreement**: The 1953 treaty that settled West Germany's old foreign debts.
- **Margin**: Borrowed money used to buy securities.
