His thinking in five minutes

Mark Minervini left school early, played music, bought his first stock in 1983 and made a quick profit on it. Then, for about six years, he kept losing money until his capital was gone. After that he studied the biggest winning stocks in history, built a method he calls SEPA, and won the U.S. Investing Championship twice, in 1997 and 2021. His trademark is a strict checklist: buy only leading stocks in strong uptrends, at a precise point, and cut every loss small.

  • Twice: U.S. Investing Champion: 1997 (+155%) and 2021 (+334.8%)
  • About 6 years: of losing money before his method worked
  • 8 tests: in his Trend Template before a stock is worth a look
  • 10%: the most he says any loss should ever reach

His thinking, one line each

  • Study the winners. Copy what the biggest winners looked like before they ran.
  • Stage 2 only. Buy only stocks already in a strong uptrend.
  • The Trend Template. Eight tests on price and averages filter out the laggards.
  • The VCP. Wait for the price swings to shrink, then buy the breakout.
  • Small losses. Cut every loss early; never let one grow past 10%.
  • Right half the time. Win by making more on winners than you lose on losers.

The big ideas

  1. A musician who lost it all. He wanted to be a musician, bought his first stock in 1983, and made money at once.
  2. Study the winners. Instead of guessing, he studied what the biggest winning stocks in history looked like before their big moves, and built a 'leadership profile' to copy.
  3. SEPA: a specific entry point. In 1993 he founded Quantech Research Group, selling research based on his method, Specific Entry Point Analysis, or SEPA.
  4. Stage 2 only. Borrowing Stan Weinstein's idea that stocks move through four stages, he buys only in Stage 2: when a stock is already in a strong, rising trend.
  5. The Trend Template. His trademark filter: eight tests on price, moving averages and the yearly high and low.
  6. The VCP. His favourite setup is the Volatility Contraction Pattern: a rising stock pauses, and each pullback is smaller than the one before, until the price breaks out.
  7. Cut losses small. After losing everything once, he made one rule above all: cut losses while they are small.
  8. 220% a year. Jack Schwager reported that from mid-1994 to 1999 he averaged about 220% a year, with only one small losing quarter.
  9. Two championships. To prove his method with real money, he entered the U.S.
  10. Teacher, critics and the CNBC moment. He now writes books and sells trading education.

His trademark: the Trend Template

Run the Trend Template

His eight tests, as published:

  1. Price above the 150-day and 200-day moving averages.
  2. The 150-day average above the 200-day.
  3. The 200-day average rising for at least a month.
  4. The 50-day average above the 150-day and 200-day.
  5. Price above the 50-day average.
  6. Price at least 25% above its 52-week low (30% in his first book).
  7. Price within 25% of its 52-week high.
  8. Relative strength rank of at least 70.

On the full site you can type in a stock's numbers and see which tests pass.

A study aid using his published criteria (the 25% version of the 52-week-low test). Passing does not make a stock a buy, and failing does not make it a sell. Not a trading signal.

The VCP in four steps

  1. A Stage 2 stock: it must already pass the Trend Template.
  2. It pauses and pulls back, then each pullback is smaller than the last.
  3. Volume dries up as the swings tighten.
  4. Buy the breakout above the last tight range, with a stop just below it.

Our summary of his books as explained by ChartMill.

Leader or laggard? Tap your answer

  1. A share is down 60% from its high and looks cheap. Laggard. He avoids stocks far from their highs; the template wants within 25% of the 52-week high.
  2. A share is 5% below its yearly high, above its rising 50-, 150- and 200-day averages. Leader. That is the Trend Template's picture of a Stage 2 leader.
  3. A share's 200-day average has been falling for six months, but it jumped 10% today. Laggard. He wants the 200-day average rising for at least a month; one good day does not change the trend.
  4. A rising share pulls back 20%, then 10%, then 4%, on lighter and lighter volume. Leader. Shrinking pullbacks on drying volume are his VCP.

Words to know

Moving average
The average closing price over a set number of days, redrawn each day.
52-week high/low
The highest and lowest prices of the past year.
Relative strength
How a stock's price has done compared with other stocks, often as a 1 to 99 rank.
Stage 2
In Stan Weinstein's scheme, the rising phase of a stock's cycle.
VCP
Volatility Contraction Pattern: a pause in which each pullback is smaller than the last.
Pivot
The price level whose breakout is the buy signal.
Stop loss
A preset price at which you sell to limit a loss.
SEPA
Specific Entry Point Analysis, his name for his whole method.
