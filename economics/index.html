The Lemonade Stand

How money works in a business, with one little stand and a lot of lemons. By the end you will read a company's three big reports like a grown-up.

Start the story ↓Jump to the games

Meet the stand

Sunny wants to earn some money this summer, so Sunny opens a lemonade stand. Keep an eye on the bar at the top of your screen: it shows Sunny's cash, the lemons waiting in the cooler and the profit so far, and it changes as the story goes on.

Opening morning

Getting ready

  • Sunny puts $30 of savings into a jar. This is the owner's money: the business now has $30.
  • Sunny buys the stand (table, sign and jug) for $12. Cash goes down to $18.
Is the $12 stand a cost today? Not all of it. The stand will be used for 3 years, so it is something Sunny owns. Each year a little of it "wears out", and only that part counts as a cost. We'll come back to this.
Day 1 · Sunny weather

The first sale!

  • Sunny buys lemons, sugar and cups for 15 glasses. Each glass costs $1 to make, so that's $15.
  • Sunny makes 12 glasses and keeps lemons for 3 more in the cooler.
  • Customers buy 10 glasses at $2 each.
  • By evening, 2 glasses in the jug are warm. Nobody wants warm lemonade, so they're poured away.

Today's result

Sales+$20
Cost of 10 sold−$10
Poured away−$2
Profit$8

Wait, profit is $8 but the jar only grew by $5? The missing $3 is sitting in the cooler as lemons. It isn't lost, just not used yet. Lemons waiting to be used are called inventory.
Day 2 · Very hot

Everyone is thirsty

  • Sunny uses the 3 glasses' worth of lemons from the cooler and buys lemons for 10 more ($10).
  • All 13 glasses sell at $2. Nothing is poured away.
  • Milo the cat forgot his money. "Can I pay next week?" Sunny says yes. Milo owes $2: that's an IOU ("I owe you").

Today's result

Sales+$26
Cost of 13 sold−$13
Poured away$0
Profit$13

Cash jar

Start$23
Paid the shop−$10
Customers paid+$24
End$37
A sale counts on the day the lemonade is sold, even if the money comes later. Milo's $2 is part of today's sales, but it's not in the jar yet. Money that others owe you is called receivables.
Day 3 · Rainy

A bad day

  • Sunny buys lemons for 8 glasses ($8) and makes all 8.
  • It rains. Only 3 glasses sell ($6).
  • Milo comes back and pays his $2 IOU. Good cat.
  • 5 glasses are left at closing time and get poured away.

Today's result

Sales+$6
Cost of 3 sold−$3
Poured away−$5
Loss−$2

Cash jar

Start$37
Paid the shop−$8
Customers paid+$6
Milo's IOU+$2
End$37
A loss, but the jar didn't shrink! Milo's $2 is cash coming in, but it is not a new sale. That sale was already counted on day 2.

The big lesson: profit is not cash

DayProfitChange in the jar
1 · sunny$8+$5
2 · hot$13+$14
3 · rainy−$2$0

They never match, because of three things:

🍋Lemons waitingCash paid now, cost counted when used
✍️IOUsSale counted now, cash comes later
🛠️Things that lastCash paid once, cost spread over years
Remember: profit tells you whether the stand is a good business. Cash tells you whether it can open again tomorrow. Plenty of real businesses with profits have closed because they ran out of cash.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between profit and cash?

Profit tells you whether the business made money over a period. Cash is the actual money in the bank or jar today. They differ because of things like unpaid bills, money customers still owe you, stock waiting to be used, and equipment that is paid for once but counted as a cost over several years.

What are the three financial statements?

The profit and loss statement (did we make money this year?), the balance sheet (what do we own and owe on one day?) and the cash flow statement (where did the cash come from and go?).

What is a balance sheet, in simple words?

A snapshot on one day of everything a business owns (assets), everything it owes (liabilities) and the owner's share (equity). What it owns always equals what it owes plus the owner's share, which is why it is called a balance sheet.

What is depreciation, simply?

Spreading the cost of something that lasts several years over those years. A 30-dollar stand that lasts 3 years counts as a 10-dollar cost each year, even though all the cash was paid at the start.

What is inventory?

Goods a business has bought but not yet sold or used, like lemons waiting in the cooler. It is counted as something the business owns until it is used.

Can a profitable business run out of cash?

Yes. If customers pay late, if too much stock is bought, or if big equipment is paid for at once, the cash can run out even while the business is making a profit.
