Chapter 1

Inflation: why prices creep up 🍋

Inflation is the rise in the general level of prices. When it is 5%, the same basket of things costs 5% more than a year ago.

Sunny's lemonade cost 10 rupees last summer. This summer lemons, sugar and cups all cost a bit more, so Sunny charges 10.50. That is 5% inflation, for one glass.

Governments measure inflation with a consumer price index (CPI): a big imaginary shopping basket of food, rent, fuel, clothes, school fees and more, priced every month.

Chapter 2

Interest rates: the price of money

Interest is rent for money. Borrowers pay it; savers earn it.

Sunny wants a bigger stand that costs 10,000 rupees. A bank lends the money, and Sunny pays it back with interest. Meanwhile Sunny's friend Pip puts savings in the same bank and earns interest. The bank sits in the middle, charging borrowers more than it pays savers.

The central bank's lever

The Reserve Bank of India sets a key rate (the repo rate) at which banks borrow from it. Raise it, and loans across the country slowly get dearer:

🏦Repo rate upRBI raises its rate
💳Loans cost moreHome and business loans
🛍️Less spendingPeople and firms borrow less
🌡️Prices coolInflation eases

Cut the rate and the chain runs the other way: cheaper loans, more spending, a faster economy, and a risk of higher prices. Remember Stanley Druckenmiller's lesson in the investors library: for the whole stock market, central banks and money flows often matter more than company profits.

Chapter 3

The rupee and the dollar 💱

An exchange rate is the price of one currency in another. "₹95 to the dollar" means one US dollar costs 95 rupees.

When Sunny imports fancy lemons from abroad, Sunny needs dollars. If the rupee weakens, those lemons get dearer in rupees even if their dollar price stays the same.

How many rupees for one dollar, 1975 to 2026

2025Actual –Inflation gap only –

India's prices rose about 28 times from 1975 to 2024, America's about 6 times. That inflation gap alone would have taken the dollar from ₹8.4 to about ₹41 by 2024. It actually reached about ₹84: the rupee also fell for other reasons, including the end in 1991 of a fixed rate that had kept it overvalued, and money flowing in and out of India.

Chapter 4

The lemonade price index

Why your rupees go further in India than they would in America.

Suppose a glass of lemonade costs ₹30 in India and $3 in America. For the glass, ₹30 buys what $3 buys, so the "lemonade exchange rate" is ₹10 per dollar. The market rate is about ₹95. At market rates, Indian lemonade looks very cheap.

This is the idea behind purchasing power parity (PPP). The Economist magazine runs the same test with burgers, its Big Mac index. Things made and sold locally, like haircuts, rent and street food, are usually much cheaper in poorer countries, because local wages are lower.

    Questions people ask

    What is inflation, in simple words?

    The rise in the general level of prices. If inflation is 5%, the same basket of everyday things costs 5% more than a year earlier.

    What is India's inflation target?

    4% consumer-price inflation, with a band of 2% to 6%, first notified on 5 August 2016 and renewed in 2026. The Reserve Bank of India sets interest rates to meet it.

    How much is 100 rupees from 1991 worth today?

    About 871 rupees in 2024 prices, based on World Bank consumer-price inflation for India.

    Why do central banks raise interest rates?

    To cool inflation. Higher rates make loans dearer, so people and companies borrow and spend less, which eases pressure on prices.

    Why has the rupee fallen against the dollar?

    Mainly because prices have risen faster in India than in America for decades, and because before 1991 a fixed exchange rate kept the rupee overvalued. A dollar cost about 8.4 rupees in 1975 and about 87 in 2025.

    What is purchasing power parity?

    A way of comparing currencies by what they actually buy. If lemonade costs 30 rupees in India and 3 dollars in America, the lemonade exchange rate is 10 rupees per dollar.
