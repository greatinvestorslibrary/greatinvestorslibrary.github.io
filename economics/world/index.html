The World Economy

One lemonade stand is a business. A whole town of them is an economy. Here is how we measure a country's economy, who has the biggest ones today, and how India and China raced each other for 50 years.

Chapter 1

What is GDP? 🍋

GDP (gross domestic product) is the value of everything a country makes and sells to final buyers in one year.

Picture a tiny town. A farmer grows lemons and sells them to Sunny's stand for $20. A mill sells Sunny sugar for $10. Sunny turns them into lemonade and sells it to thirsty customers for $60.

Count the town's economy

Tap the two ways of counting. Which one is right?

Each business adds something: the farmer adds $20, the mill $10, and Sunny's squeezing, mixing and selling adds $30. Add those up and you get $60, the same as the final price. Economists call each step value added.

🛒SpendingWhat people, companies and the government spend on final goods, plus exports minus imports
💵IncomeWages, profits and rents everyone earns from making those goods
🏭ProductionThe value added at every step, from farm to shop

Three ways, one answer. Every rupee spent on lemonade is a rupee earned by someone who helped make it, so all three ways should give the same GDP. In real life the numbers differ a little, and statisticians reconcile them.

To compare how well off people are, we divide GDP by the number of people. That is GDP per person (or per capita). A town that makes $60 with 3 people makes $20 per person.

Big is not the same as rich

India's economy (about $4.06 trillion) is almost as big as the UK's ($4.27 trillion). But India has about 1.48 billion people and the UK about 70 million, so the UK makes about 22 times more per person.

Why "at local prices"?

A glass of lemonade, a haircut or a bus ride costs far less in India than in America. Converting rupees to dollars at the market rate makes Indian incomes look smaller than what they actually buy at home. Purchasing power parity (PPP) adjusts for this, so India's GDP per person rises from $2,750 to about $13,000 in "international dollars".

    What the race shows

    🤝A close startIn 1978 China's economy was $150 billion and India's $137 billion. In 1987 India was even slightly ahead in dollars.
    🔁A surpriseFrom 1977 to 1990, the average Indian had a higher income in dollars than the average Chinese.
    🚀The great divergenceBy 2023 China's economy was about 5 times India's. In dollars it grew about 11% a year from 1978, India about 7.5%.
    ⏱️MilestonesIndia passed $1 trillion in 2007, $2 trillion in 2014 and $3 trillion in 2021. China passed $1 trillion in 1998.

    Why did China pull ahead? Economists still argue. Common explanations include very high saving and investment, a huge push into factories making goods for export, and an earlier start on reforms (1978, against India's 1991). India's growth has been faster than China's in recent years, which is why the race is not over.

    Chapter 5

    What GDP misses

    GDP is the best single measure of an economy's size, but it is not a measure of happiness.

    🍳Unpaid workCooking for your family counts as zero. Hire a cook to make the same meal and GDP goes up.
    ⚖️Who gets whatAn average hides inequality. A few very rich people can lift GDP per person while most incomes stay flat.
    🌳NatureCutting down a forest to sell the wood adds to GDP. The lost forest is not subtracted.
    🧾Hidden tradeCash work that is never recorded is only partly estimated, and in some countries it is large.

      Questions people ask

      What is GDP, in simple words?

      GDP is the value of all the final goods and services a country makes in a year. Only final sales count: the lemons inside a glass of lemonade are not counted again on their own.

      Which is the biggest economy in the world in 2026?

      The United States, at about $32.6 trillion, followed by China at about $21.5 trillion, according to the IMF's October 2026 estimates at market exchange rates. Measured at local prices (PPP), China is the largest.

      What is GDP per capita?

      GDP divided by the number of people. It is an average of output per person, not what a typical person earns.

      What is PPP?

      Purchasing power parity adjusts for the fact that the same things cost different amounts in different countries, so it compares what money actually buys at local prices.

      When did China's economy overtake India's?

      In US dollars the two were close until the late 1980s; India was even slightly ahead in 1987 by World Bank figures. China pulled away from the 1990s and was about five times India's size by 2023.

      What is the rule of 70?

      Divide 70 by a growth rate to estimate how many years it takes to double. At 7% a year, an economy doubles in about 10 years.
